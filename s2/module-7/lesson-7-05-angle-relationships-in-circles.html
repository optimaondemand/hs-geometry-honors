📚 VOCABULARY

Arc Measure: The degree measure of an arc, which is equal to the measure of its corresponding central angle.

Center: The fixed point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides are radii.

Chord: A segment whose endpoints lie on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

Exterior Angle: An angle formed outside the circle by two secants, two tangents, or a secant and a tangent.

Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords.

Intercepted Arc: The arc between two points where an angle intersects the circle.

Major Arc: An arc with a measure greater than 180°.

Minor Arc: An arc with a measure less than 180°.

Point of Tangency: The single point where a tangent touches a circle.

Radius: A segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the relationships between angles formed by different segments and lines in a circle. These include angles formed by two chords, two secants, two tangents, a secant and a tangent, and a chord and a tangent. By using key theorems, we can determine the measures of these angles when given certain arc or segment information. Understanding these relationships is essential for solving geometric problems involving circles, both in mathematics and in real-world applications.


Angle Relationships in Circles

Intersecting Chords Theorem

Tangent-Chord Angle Theorem

When two chords intersect inside a circle, the measure of the angle formed is equal to half the sum of the measures of the intercepted arcs.

\(m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(Arc1+Arc2)\)

When a chord and a tangent meet at the point of tangency, the angle formed is half the measure of the intercepted arc.

\(m\angle = \frac{1}{2}(Intercepted \; Arc)\)

Example

\(m\angle BDF=\frac{1}{2}(100^\circ +59^\circ )=79.5^\circ \)

\(\angle BDF \cong \angle CFE\) - Vertical Angles

Example

\(m\angle IHJ=\frac{1}{2}(231^\circ )=115.5^\circ \)


Example 1

In \(\odot A\), chords \(\overline{BC}\) and \(\overline{DE}\) intersect at point \(F\).

  • \(m\overset{\frown}{DB}=84^\circ \)
  • \(m\angle CFE=72^\circ \).

Find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{CE}\).

Solution:

  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{CE}\), we will use the intersecting chords theorem.
  • \(m\angle CFE=\frac{1}{2}(m\overset{\frown}{DB}+m\overset{\frown}{CE})\)
  • We will substitute the values we know and solve for what we do not know.
  • \(72^\circ =\frac{1}{2}(84^\circ  +m\overset{\frown}{CE})\)
    \(2\cdot 72^\circ =2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(84^\circ  +m\overset{\frown}{CE})\)
    \(144^\circ = 84^\circ + m\overset{\frown}{CE}\)
    \(144^\circ - 84^\circ = m\overset{\frown}{CE}\)

We find that \(m\overset{\frown}{CE}=60^\circ \).


Example 2

In \(\odot A\), chords \(\overline{BD}\) and \(\overline{CE}\) intersect at point \(F\).

  • \(m\overset{\frown}{BC}=(3x+10)^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{DE}=(5x-4)^\circ \)
  • \(m\angle BFC=74^\circ \)

Find the value of x and the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\).

Solution:

  • To find the value of \(x\) and measure of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\), we will use the intersecting chords theorem.
  • \(m\angle BFC=\frac{1}{2}(m\overset{\frown}{BC}+m\overset{\frown}{DE})\)
  • We will substitute the values and algebraic expressions into the formula and solve for x.
  • \(74^\circ =\frac{1}{2}((3x+10)^\circ  + (5x-4)^\circ)\)
    \(2\cdot 74^\circ =2\cdot \frac{1}{2}((3x+10)^\circ  + (5x-4)^\circ)\)
    \(148 = 3x+10 + 5x-4\)
    \(148 = 8x+6\)
    \(148-6=8x-6\)
    \(142=8x\)
    \(\frac{142}{8}=\frac{8x}{8}\)
  • \(x=17.75\)
  • Now, we substitute the value of x into the expression for \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\) to find its measure.
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{BC}=(3x+10)^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{BC}=(3(17.75)+10)^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{BC}=(53.25+10)^\circ \)

We find that \(x=17.75\) and \(m\overset{\frown}{BC}=63.25^\circ \).


Example 3

In \(\odot G\), tangent \(\overline{HJ}\) and chord \(\overline{HI}\) meet at the point of tangency \(H\).

  • \(m\overset{\frown}{HI}=130^\circ \)
  • \(m\angle IHJ=(2x+5)^\circ \)

Find the value of \(x\).

Solution:

  • To find the value of \(x\), we will use the tangent-chord angle theorem.
  • \(m\angle IHJ=\frac{1}{2}(m\overset{\frown}{HI})\)
  • We will substitute the values and algebraic expressions into the formula and solve for x.
  • \((2x+5)^\circ =\frac{1}{2}(130^\circ)\)
    \((2x+5)^\circ=65^\circ \)
    \(2x+5-5=65-5\)
    \(2x=60\)
    \(\frac{2x}{2}=\frac{60}{2}\)
  • \(x=30\)

We find that \(x=30\).

 

Exterior Angle Theorems for Circles

Angle Formed by Two Secants

When two secants intersect outside a circle, the angle formed is half the difference of the intercepted arcs.

\(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\)

Angle Formed by Two Tangents

When two tangents meet at an external point, the angle formed is half the difference of the intercepted arcs.

\(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\)

Angle Formed by a Secant and a Tangent

When a secant and a tangent intersect outside a circle, the angle formed is half the difference of the intercepted arcs.

\(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\)

Example

\(m\angle RST=\frac{1}{2}(110^\circ -50^\circ )=30^\circ \)

Example

\(m\angle MNO=\frac{1}{2}(242^\circ -118^\circ )=62^\circ \)

Example

\(m\angle ZAC=\frac{1}{2}(146^\circ - 74^\circ )=36^\circ \)



Example 4

In \(\odot A\), secants \(\overline{DC}\) and \(\overline{BC}\) intersect at point \(C\).

  • \(m\angle ECF=(3x+6)^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{EF}=51^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{DB}=(10x+35)^\circ \)

Find the value of x and the measure of \(\angle ECF\).

Solution:

  • We will begin by using the exterior angles theorem for when two secants intersect outside the circle; \(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\).
  • We will substitute the values of the angle and arc measures into the formula to create an equation.
    • \((3x+6)^\circ = \frac{1}{2}((10x+35)^\circ - 51^\circ )\)
  • To make the equation easier, we will multiply both sides by 2 so we are not dealing with fractions.
    • \(2 \cdot (3x+6)^\circ = 2\cdot \frac{1}{2}((10x+35)^\circ - 51^\circ )\)
      \(6x+12=10x+35-51\)
      \(6x+12=10x-16\)
      \(12+16=10x-6x\)
      \(28=4x\)
      \(\frac{28}{4}=\frac{4x}{4}\)
  • We find that \(x=7\).
  • Now, that we have the value of x, we can substitute this into the expression for the angle and find the measure of the angle.
    • \(m\angle ECF = (3(7)+6)^\circ \implies m\angle ECF=(21+6)^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\angle ECF = 27^\circ \).



Example 5

In \(\odot I\), tangents \(\overline{KL}\) and \(\overline{KJ}\) intersect at point \(K\).

  • \(m\angle LKJ=43^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{LJ}=(15x-5.5)^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{LMJ}=(20x+33)^\circ \)

Find the value of x and the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{LMJ}\).

Solution:

  • We will begin by using the exterior angles theorem for when two tangents intersect outside the circle; \(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\).
  • We will substitute the values of the angle and arc measures into the formula to create an equation.
    • \(43^\circ = \frac{1}{2}((20x+33)^\circ - (15x-5.5)^\circ )\)
  • To make the equation easier, we will multiply both sides by 2 so we are not dealing with fractions.
    • \(2 \cdot 43^\circ = 2\cdot \frac{1}{2}((20x+33)^\circ - (15x-5.5)^\circ )\)
      \(86=20x+33-(15x-5.5)\)
      \(86=5x+38.5\)
      \(86-38.5=5x\)
      \(\frac{47.5}{5}=\frac{5x}{5}\)
  • We find that \(x=9.5\).
  • Now, that we have the value of x, we can substitute this into the expression for the larger arc to find its measure.
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{LMJ}=(20(9.5)+33)^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{LMJ}=(190+33)^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\overset{\frown}{LMJ}=223^\circ \).



Example 6

In \(\odot N\), tangent \(\overline{PQ}\) and secant \(\overline{PO}\) intersect at point \(P\).

  • \(m\angle QPR=(2x-5.5)^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{QR}=(4x-3)^\circ \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{QO}=(5x+25)^\circ \)

Find the value of x and the measure of \(\angle QPR\).

Solution:

  • We will begin by using the exterior angles theorem for when a tangent and secant intersect outside the circle; \(m\angle =\frac{1}{2}(Larger\; Arc - Smaller \; Arc)\).
  • We will substitute the values of the angle and arc measures into the formula to create an equation.
    • \((2x-5.5)^\circ = \frac{1}{2}((5x+25)^\circ - (4x-3)^\circ )\)
  • To make the equation easier, we will multiply both sides by 2 so we are not dealing with fractions.
    • \(2 \cdot (2x-5.5)^\circ = 2\cdot \frac{1}{2}((5x+25)^\circ - (4x-3)^\circ )\)
      \(4x-11=5x+25-(4x-3)\)
      \(4x-11=x+28\)
      \(4x-x=28+11\)
      \(3x=39\)
      \(\frac{3x}{3}=\frac{39}{3}\)
  • We find that \(x=13\).
  • Now, that we have the value of x, we can substitute this into the expression for the angle to find its measure.
    • \(m\angle QPR=(2(13)-5.5)^\circ \implies m\angle QPR=(26-5.5)^\circ \)
  • We find that \(m\angle QPR=20.5^\circ \).



Conclusion

Understanding the relationships between angles formed by two chords, two secants, two tangents, a secant and a tangent, and a chord and a tangent allows us to calculate unknown angle measures using geometric properties. The Intersecting Chords Theorem helps us determine angles inside a circle, while the Exterior Angle Theorem applies to angles formed outside the circle. The Tangent-Chord Theorem provides insight into angles formed at points of tangency. By applying these theorems and postulates, you will be able to solve a variety of geometric problems involving circles. Keep practicing with different diagrams and problems to strengthen your understanding!
