📚 VOCABULARY

Arc: A portion of a circle’s circumference between two points.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle and whose sides are radii.

Chord: A segment whose endpoints lie on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Cyclic Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on the circle.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle and is the longest chord.

Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords.

Inscribed Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on a circle, also called a cyclic quadrilateral.

Inscribed Triangle: A triangle with all three vertices on a circle.

Intercepted Arc: The arc that lies between the two endpoints of an angle on a circle.

Opposite Angles: Two angles in a quadrilateral that are not adjacent to each other.

Radius: A segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore inscribed triangles and inscribed quadrilaterals, their properties, and how to solve problems involving them. An inscribed triangle is a triangle where all three vertices lie on a circle. We will examine special types of inscribed triangles, including right triangles, equilateral triangles, and triangles that share intercepted arcs. Then, we will explore inscribed quadrilaterals, also known as cyclic quadrilaterals, and use their angle properties to solve for missing values both numerically and algebraically. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to apply key theorems to solve real-world and mathematical problems involving inscribed polygons.


Right Triangles Inscribed in a Circle

A right triangle can be inscribed in a circle if and only if the hypotenuse is the diameter of the circle.

This follows from the Right Triangle Theorem for Inscribed Triangles: If one side of an inscribed triangle is the diameter, then the triangle is a right triangle, and its angle opposite the diameter is a right angle.

Properties of :

  • \(b^2+c^2=d^2\)
  • \(\angle CDB\) is a right angle; \(m\angle CDB=90^\circ\)
  • \(m\angle BCD + m\angle CBD = 90^\circ\)




Equilateral Triangles Inscribed in a Circle

An equilateral triangle can also be inscribed in a circle, meaning all three vertices lie on the circle.

Since all interior angles in an equilateral triangle are 60°, we can use the Inscribed Angle Theorem and arc properties to solve for missing values.

Properties of \(\bigtriangleup FGH\):

  • All sides are congruent: \(\overline{FG} \cong \overline{GH} \cong \overline{HF}\)
  • All angles are congruent: \(\angle F \cong \angle G \cong \angle H\)
  • All angles equal \(60^\circ\).
  • Intercepted arcs equal \(120^\circ\)
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{FG}=2 \cdot m\angle H = 120^\circ\)




Two Inscribed Triangles Sharing the Same Arc

If two triangles are inscribed in a circle and share the same intercepted arc, then they have equal inscribed angles by the Congruent Inscribed Angles Theorem.

\(\bigtriangleup LNK \sim \bigtriangleup JMN\)

\(\angle LNK \cong \angle JNM\) - Vertical Angles

\(\angle L \cong \angle J\) since they intercept the same arc \(\overset{\frown}{KM}\) \(\angle K \cong \angle M\) since they intercept the same arc \(\overset{\frown}{JL}\)



Example 1

In the given circle, \(\overline{BC}\) is a diameter and \(\bigtriangleup BCD\) is inscribed in the circle as shown.

Find the values of \(x\), \(y\), and \(z\). Be sure to include the unit of measure when appropriate.

Solution:

  • We can begin by finding the value of \(x^\circ\).
  • Since \(\overline{BC}\) is a diameter, we know that the triangle is a right triangle.
  • The angle opposite the diameter is the right angle. Therefore, \(x=90^\circ\).
  • Next, we can find the value of \(y^\circ\).
  • The two acute angles in a right triangle have a sum of \(90^\circ\).
  • Therefore, \(y=90-40 \implies y=50^\circ\).
  • Last, we will find the value of \(z\). Since this is a right triangle, we can use the Pythagorean Theorem and 13 is the hypotenuse.
  • \(5^2+z^2=13^2 \implies 25+z^2=169 \implies z^2=169-25 \implies z^2=144 \implies z=\sqrt{144} \implies z=12\)


Example 2

In the circle, \(\bigtriangleup QRT\) and \(\bigtriangleup PST\) are inscribed in the circle as shown.

Find the values of \(x\) and \(y\). Be sure to include the unit of measure.

Solution:

  • We can solve for \(x^\circ\) first. We know that \(\angle RSP\) and \(\angle RQP\) intercept the same arc \(\overset{\frown}{PR}\).
  • Therefore, \(m\angle RSP = m\angle RQP\).
  • \(x=38^\circ\)
  • Next, we know that \(m\angle QTR=109^\circ\) as it is a vertical angle to \(\angle STP\). All vertical angles are congruent.
  • We also know that all the angles in a triangle equal \(180^\circ\). So, to find the value of \(y^\circ\), we can add the two angles we know and subtract from 180.
  • \(y+38+109=180 \implies y+147=180 \implies y=180-147 \implies y=33^\circ\)





Inscribed Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is inscribed in a circle if all four of its vertices lie on the circle.

This is called a cyclic quadrilateral, and it has a special property: The opposite angles of an inscribed quadrilateral are supplementary.

In quadrilateral \(BCDE\):

  • Opposite Angles are Supplementary
  • \(m\angle B + m\angle D = 180^\circ\) 
    • \(107^\circ + 73^\circ = 180^\circ\)
  • \(m\angle C + m\angle E = 180^\circ\)
    • \(91^\circ + 89^\circ = 180^\circ \)


Example 3

In \(\odot F\), quadrilateral \(GHIJ\) is inscribed in the circle.

Find the values of x and y. Then find the measures of the angles in the quadrilateral.

Solution:

  • Since the quadrilateral is inscribed in the circle, we know that opposite angles are supplementary.
  • To find the value of x, we can begin by creating an equation of the sum of the two angle measures equal to 180.
  • \(m\angle G + m\angle I = 180^\circ \implies 3x+4.2x=180 \implies 7.2x=180 \implies x=180\div 7.2 \implies x=25\)
  • Now, we can use the value of x to find the measures of \(\angle G\) and \(\angle I\).
  • \(m\angle G = 3(25) = 75^\circ\) and \(m\angle I=4.2(25) = 105^\circ \)
  • Next, to find y, we will create a similar equation with \(\angle H\) and \(\angle J\).
  • \(m\angle H+m\angle J=180^\circ \implies 26y+10y=180 \implies 36y=180 \implies y=180\div 36 \implies y=5\).
  • Now, we can find the measures of \(\angle H\) and \(\angle J\).
  • \(m\angle H=26(5) = 130^\circ\) and \(m\angle J = 10(5) = 50^\circ\)


Conclusion

Understanding the properties of inscribed triangles and inscribed quadrilaterals allows us to solve a variety of geometric problems. The Right Triangle Theorem applies when a triangle’s hypotenuse is the diameter, and the Inscribed Angle Theorem helps determine angle measures in triangles and quadrilaterals. The Cyclic Quadrilateral Theorem states that opposite angles in an inscribed quadrilateral are supplementary, which is useful when solving for missing values numerically and algebraically. By practicing these concepts, you will strengthen your ability to analyze inscribed polygons in circles and apply these principles to real-world problems.
