📚 VOCABULARY

Arc: A portion of a circle’s circumference between two points.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides are radii.

Chord: A segment whose endpoints lie on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Compass: A tool used to draw circles and arcs.

Cyclic Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose vertices all lie on a circle.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle and is the longest chord.

Hexagon: A six-sided polygon.

Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords.

Inscribed Hexagon: A hexagon where all six vertices lie on a circle.

Inscribed Polygon: A polygon where all of its vertices lie on a circle.

Inscribed Quadrilateral: A quadrilateral whose four vertices all lie on a circle.

Inscribed Triangle: A triangle where all three vertices lie on a circle.

Intercepted Arc: The arc between two points where an angle intersects the circle.

Perpendicular Bisector: A line that divides a segment into two equal parts at a 90-degree angle.

Radius: A segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Straightedge: A tool used to draw straight lines without measurement markings.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore inscribed regular polygons, specifically the equilateral triangle (regular triangle), square (regular quadrilateral), and regular hexagon. A regular polygon is a polygon with all sides and angles equal. When a regular polygon is inscribed in a circle, all of its vertices lie on the circle’s circumference, and the polygon maintains symmetry about the center of the circle.

We will first define the properties and relationships of inscribed regular polygons and then learn the step-by-step process for constructing:

  1. An inscribed equilateral triangle
  2. An inscribed square
  3. An inscribed regular hexagon

By the end of this lesson, you will understand how to construct these shapes using a compass and straightedge and how they relate to the properties of circles.

 

Definitions and Properties of Inscribed Regular Polygons

Inscribed Regular Polygon

An inscribed regular polygon is a regular polygon where all vertices lie on the circle, and all sides are equal in length. The center of the circle is also the center of the polygon, making the polygon symmetric about the center.

Each inscribed regular polygon has special properties that relate its angles and arcs to the circle.


Inscribed Equilateral Triangle (Regular Triangle)

  • An equilateral triangle inscribed in a circle has all three sides equal and all three angles equal to 60°.
  • Each central angle (formed by radii connecting consecutive vertices to the center) measures 120°.
  • The perpendicular bisectors of the triangle’s sides intersect at the circumcenter, which is also the center of the circle.

Inscribed Square (Regular Quadrilateral)

  • A square inscribed in a circle has four equal sides and four right angles (90° each).
  • Each central angle (formed by radii connecting consecutive vertices to the center) measures 90°.
  • The diagonals of the square are diameters of the circle and bisect each other at 90°.

Inscribed Regular Hexagon

  • A regular hexagon inscribed in a circle consists of six equal sides and six equal angles (each measuring 120°).
  • Each central angle (formed by radii connecting consecutive vertices to the center) measures 60°.
  • The hexagon is composed of six equilateral triangles, each with a side length equal to the radius of the circle.



Constructing an Inscribed Equilateral Triangle (Regular Triangle)

  1. Draw a Circle

    • Use a compass to draw a circle with a chosen center and radius.

  2. Use the Compass to Find the Other Vertices

    • Open the compass to width of the radius using the center and the given point on the circle.
      • This given point on the circle will be the first vertex of the triangle.
    • Keep the compass width equal to the radius of the circle.
    • Place the compass at the first vertex and mark an arc on the circle.
    • Move the compass to the new point and mark another arc.
    • Repeat this step until you have 6 arcs on the circl.
    • Starting with the first vertex that was given, use three equally spaced points around the circle.
      • This will be every other arc mark on the circle.

  3. Connect the Vertices

    • Use a straightedge to draw line segments connecting the three points, forming an equilateral triangle inscribed in the circle.

Constructing an Inscribed Square (Regular Quadrilateral)

  1. Draw a Circle

    • Use a compass to draw a circle with a chosen center and radius.

  2. Draw the Diameter

    • Use a straightedge to draw a diameter, which is a straight line passing through the center of the circle.
    • This creates two opposite points on the circle that will be two vertices of the square.

  3. Draw the Perpendicular Diameter

    • Using a compass and straightedge, construct a perpendicular bisector of the diameter.
    • This will create two more points on the circle, which will be the remaining two vertices of the square.

  4. Connect the Four Points

    • Use a straightedge to draw line segments between the four points, forming a square inscribed in the circle.

Constructing an Inscribed Regular Hexagon

  1. Draw a Circle

    • Use a compass to draw a circle with a chosen center and radius.

  2. Mark the First Vertex

    • Identify the given point on the circle as the first vertex of the hexagon.

  3. Use the Compass to Find the Other Five Vertices

    • Open the compass to width of the radius using the center and the given point on the circle.
      • This given point on the circle will be the first vertex of the hexagon.
    • Keep the compass width equal to the radius of the circle.
    • Place the compass at the first vertex and mark an arc on the circle.
    • Move the compass to the new point and mark another arc.
    • Continue this process until you have six equally spaced points around the circle.

  4. Connect the Vertices

    • Use a straightedge to draw line segments connecting the six points in order, forming a regular hexagon inscribed in the circle.

Conclusion

Understanding inscribed regular polygons, such as the equilateral triangle, square, and regular hexagon, helps us see how polygons interact with circles. These polygons maintain symmetry, and their properties, such as central angles, intercepted arcs, and perpendicular bisectors, provide a geometric foundation for their construction.

By using a compass and straightedge, we can accurately construct these inscribed regular polygons, ensuring equal side lengths and angles. Mastering these constructions strengthens geometric problem-solving skills and has applications in architecture, engineering, and design. Keep practicing these constructions to build confidence in geometric reasoning and precision!
