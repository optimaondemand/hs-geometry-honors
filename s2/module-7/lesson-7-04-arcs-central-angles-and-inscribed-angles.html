Arcs, Central Angles, and Inscribed Angles
Lesson
📚 VOCABULARY

Arc Measure: The degree measure of an arc, which is equal to the measure of its corresponding central angle.

Center: The fixed point in a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides are radii.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords.

Intercepted Arc: The arc between two points where an angle intersects the circle.

Major Arc: An arc with a measure greater than 180°.

Minor Arc: An arc with a measure less than 180°.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Semicircle: An arc that measures exactly 180° and is formed when a diameter divides the circle in half.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the relationships between central angles, inscribed angles, and their corresponding intercepted arcs. A central angle is an angle whose vertex is at the center of the circle, and its measure is equal to the measure of the intercepted arc. An inscribed angle is an angle whose vertex is on the circle, and its measure is half the measure of its intercepted arc. We will also learn how to calculate arc and angle measures using the Arc Addition Postulate. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to solve problems involving central angles, inscribed angles, and arcs in a given circle.


Central Angles and Their Intercepted Arcs

Central Angle Theorem

The measures of a central angle and its intercepted arc are equal.

Example:

In \(\odot A\), central angle \(m\angle BAC=70^\circ \) and the intercepted arc \(m\overset{\frown}{BC}=70^\circ\).

Finding Arc or Angle Measures Given One

  • If you are given a central angle, its intercepted arc has the same measure.
  • If you are given the arc measure, the central angle is equal to the arc.



Using the Arc Addition Postulate

The Arc Addition Postulate states that the measure of an arc formed by two adjacent arcs is the sum of the measures of those arcs.

Example:

In \(\odot I\), the measures of \(m\overset{\frown}{JK}=92^\circ \) and \(m\overset{\frown}{KL}=96^\circ \). Using arc addition postulate \(m\overset{\frown}{JKL}=m\overset{\frown}{JK}+m\overset{\frown}{KL} \implies m\overset{\frown}{JKL}=92^\circ + 96^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{JKL}=188^\circ \)



Example 1

In \(\odot D\), \(m\angle EDF=84^\circ\) and \(m\overset{\frown}{FG}=123^\circ \).

Find the following measures:

  • \(m\overset{\frown}{EF}\)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{EFG}\)
  • \(m\angle FDG\)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{GHE}\)

Solution:

  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{EF}\) we will use the central angle theorem. The measure of an intercepted arc is equal to the central angle.
    • \(\overset{\frown}{EF}=84^\circ \)
  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{EFG}\) we will use the arc addition postulate. The sum of two adjacent arcs equals the larger arc.
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{EFG}=m\overset{\frown}{EF}+m\overset{\frown}{FG} \implies m\overset{\frown}{EFG}=84^\circ + 123^\circ \implies \overset{\frown}{EFG}=207^\circ \)
  • To find the measure of \(\angle FDG\) we will use the reverse of the same theorem. The measure of a central angle is equal to its intercepted arc.
    • \(m\angle FDG=123^\circ \)
  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{GHE}\) we will use the angle addition postulate and the fact that a circle equals \(360^\circ \).
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{GHE}=360^\circ - m\overset{\frown}{EFG} \implies m\overset{\frown}{GHE}=360^\circ - 207^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{GHE}=153^\circ \)




Inscribed Angles and Their Intercepted Arcs

Inscribed Angle Theorem

The measure of an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc.

Example:

in \(\odot M\), \(m\overset{\frown}{NP}=66^\circ \) and \(m\angle NOP=\frac{1}{2}m\overset{\frown}{NP} \implies m\angle NOP=33^\circ \).


Inscribed Angles Intercepting the Same Arc Theorem

If two inscribed angles intercept the same arc, they have equal measures.

Example:

In \(\odot M\), \(m\overset{\frown}{NP}=66^\circ \) and \(m\angle NOP=m\angle NQP=\frac{1}{2}m\overset{\frown}{NP}=33^\circ \).



Example 2

In \(\odot R\), \(m\angle STU=27^\circ \) and \(m\overset{\frown}{UW}=106^\circ \).

Find the following measures:

  • \(m\overset{\frown}{US}\)
  • \(m\angle UVW \)
  • \(m\overset{\frown}{WVS}\)

Solution:

  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{US}\) we will use the inscribed angle theorem. The measure of an intercepted arc is double the inscribed angle.
    • \(\overset{\frown}{US}=2 \cdot 27^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{US}=54^\circ \)
  • To find the measure of \(\angle UVW\) we will use the inscribed angle theorem. The measure of the inscribed angle is half the measure of the intercepted arc.
    • \(m\angle UVW=\frac{1}{2}m\overset{\frown}{UW} \implies m\angle UVW=\frac{1}{2}(106^\circ) \implies m\angle UVW=53^\circ \)
  • To find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{WVS}\) we will use the angle addition postulate and the fact that a circle equals \(360^\circ \).
  • We need to fist find the measure of \(\overset{\frown}{SUW}\) using the angle addition postulate.
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{SUW}=m\overset{\frown}{SU} + \overset{\frown}{UW} \implies  m\overset{\frown}{SUW}=54^\circ + 106^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{SUW}=160^\circ \)
    • \(m\overset{\frown}{WVS}=360^\circ - m\overset{\frown}{SUW} \implies m\overset{\frown}{WVS}=360^\circ - 160^\circ \implies m\overset{\frown}{GHE}=200^\circ \)



Conclusion

Understanding the relationships between central angles, inscribed angles, and their intercepted arcs helps us solve various geometric problems involving circles. The Arc Addition Postulate allows us to calculate arc measures by adding or subtracting given arc values. The Inscribed Angle Theorem states that an inscribed angle is half the measure of its intercepted arc, while a central angle is equal to its intercepted arc. These properties are useful in both mathematics and real-world applications, such as in engineering, navigation, and design. Keep practicing these concepts by solving problems and exploring their applications in real life!
