📚 VOCABULARY

Arc (in a construction): A portion of a circle drawn using a compass, typically used to locate points or measure distances in geometric constructions.

Center: The fixed point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Compass: A geometric tool used to draw circles and arcs by maintaining a fixed radius.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

Perpendicular: Two lines or segments that intersect at a 90° angle.

Point of Tangency: The single point where a tangent touches a circle.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.

Straightedge: A tool used to draw straight lines, typically without measurement markings.

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point without crossing it.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to construct a tangent to a circle from a point outside the circle using a compass and straightedge. A tangent is a line that touches a circle at exactly one point, called the point of tangency. Understanding the properties of tangents is essential for creating accurate constructions. Specifically, we will use the fact that a radius is always perpendicular to the tangent at the point of tangency. By following a step-by-step construction method, you will be able to create a tangent line to a given circle from a point outside of it.


Steps to Construct a Tangent to a Circle from an External Point

 

  1. Connect the External Point to the Circle’s Center

    • Use a straightedge to draw a line from the external point to the center of the circle.

  2. Find the Perpendicular Bisector

    • Use a compass to find the midpoint of the segment connecting the external point and the center.
    • To do this, place the compass at one endpoint of the segment and draw an arc. Without changing the compass width, repeat from the other endpoint. The two arcs will intersect at two points.
    • Use a straightedge to connect these intersection points, forming the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

  3. Draw a Circle with the Midpoint as the Center

    • Set the compass to the distance from the midpoint to the external point.
    • Draw a circle centered at the midpoint, passing through both the external point and the original circle’s center.

  4. Find the Points of Tangency

    • The new circle will intersect the given circle at two points. These are the points of tangency.

  5. Draw the Tangents

    • Use a straightedge to draw a line from the external point to each of the two points of tangency. These are the two possible tangent lines to the circle.

Conclusion

Understanding how to construct a tangent from an external point to a circle is an important skill in geometry. By using a compass and straightedge, you can apply the properties of tangents and perpendicular bisectors to create accurate constructions. This method relies on the fact that a tangent is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. Mastering this construction will help you develop a deeper understanding of circles, tangents, and geometric reasoning. Keep practicing to refine your skills and gain confidence in your constructions!
