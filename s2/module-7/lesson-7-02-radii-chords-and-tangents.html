📚 VOCABULARY

Center: The point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

External Segment: The portion of a secant segment that lies outside the circle, extending from an external point to the circle's boundary.

Internal Segment: The portion of a secant segment that lies inside the circle, extending from the point where the secant enters the circle to the second intersection point.

Point of Tangency: The point at which a tangent line touches the circle.

Radius: A line segment from the center of the circle to any point on the circle.

Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.

Segment: A part of a line that has two endpoints and a measurable length. In a circle, segments can include chords, secant segments, radii, and diameters.

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point without crossing it.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to solve problems involving the lengths of secants, tangents, and chords. These segments and lines are crucial components of circles, and understanding their relationships allows us to tackle both geometric and real-world problems. We will use key theorems, such as the Intersecting Chords Theorem, the Two Secants Theorem, and the Secant-Tangent Theorem, to find unknown lengths of these segments in given diagrams. By the end of this lesson, you will not only be able to calculate these lengths but also understand the mathematical relationships that connect them.

Diameter-Chord Perpendicularity Theorem

If a diameter is perpendicular to a chord, then it bisects the chord into two equal parts. If one chord is a perpendicular bisector of another chord, then the first chord is a diameter.

We know that \(\overline{WU} \cong \overline{VU}\) and \(\overline{WR} \cong \overline{VR}\)

We know that \(\overline{TS}\) is a diameter.

 

Example 1

In the given diagram, circle \(M\) has a radius \(\overline{MN}\), and \(\overline{NO}\) is a tangent to the circle at point \(N\).

Given: \(MN=6\) and \(NO=8\)

Find: The length of \(\overline{MO}\)

Solution:

  • Since a radius is always perpendicular to a tangent at the point of tangency, we have a right triangle \(\bigtriangleup MNO\)
  • Since \(\bigtriangleup MNO\) is a right triangle, we can use the Pythagorean Theorem to solve for the length of \(\overline{MO}\).
  • The segement \(\overline{MO}\) would be consider the hypotenuse and \(\overline{MN}\) and \(\overline{NO}\) will be considered the legs.
  • \(MO^2=MN^2+NO^2\)
  • We can substitute the values into the equation.
  • \(MO^2=(6)^2+(8)^2 \implies MO^2=36+64 \implies MO^2=100 \implies MO=\sqrt{100}=10\)

The length of \(\overline{MO}\) is 10 units.



Example 2

In the given diagram, circle \(M\) has diameter \(\overline{VW}\) and radius \(\overline{MN}\).

\(VW=4x+10\) and \(MN=3x+2\).

Find the value of x and the length of the radius.

Solution:

  • We can use the relationship between the radius and the diameter to solve for \(x\).
  • The diameter is twice the length of the radius.
  • \(VW=2\cdot MN\)
  • We can substitute the expressions into the equation.
  • \(4x+10=2(3x+2) \implies 4x+10=6x+4 \implies 4x-4x+10=6x-4x+4 \implies 10=2x+4 \implies 10-4=2x+4-4 \implies 6=2x \implies \frac{6}{2}=\frac{2x}{2} \implies x=3\)
  • Now that we know the value of x, we can substitute this into the expression for \(MN\) to find the length of the radius.
  • \(MN=3(3)+2 \implies MN=9+2 \implies MN=11\)

The value of \(x\) is 3 and the length of \(\overline{MN}\) is 11 units.



Example 3

In the given diagram, circle \(A\) has chords \(\overline{DE}\) and \(\overline{BC}\) that intersect at point \(F\).

\(DF=4.8\), \(FE=x\), \(BF=5\), and \(FC=2.3\).

Find the value of x.

 

Solution:

  • We can use the intersecting chords theorem: When two chords intersect inside a circle, the product of the segments of one chord is equal to the product of the segments of the other chord.
  • We have two chords \(\overline{DE}\) and \(\overline{BC}\) that intersect at point \(F\), we can set up an equation to solve for \(x\).
  • \(DF \cdot EF=BF \cdot CF\)
  • We can substitute the values into the equation.
  • \(4.8 \cdot x=5 \cdot 2.3 \implies 4.8x=11.5 \implies \frac{4.8x}{4.8}=\frac{11.5}{4.8} \implies x=2.3958\).
  • We can round to the nearest tenth to match the format of the other numbers.

The value of \(x\) is 2.4.



Example 4

In the given diagram, the circle has chords \(\overline{WV}\) and \(\overline{TS}\) that intersect at point \(U\).

\(\overline{WU}\cong \overline{VU}\), \(WU=4\), \(US=2\), and \(TS=10\).

Find the length of \(\overline{TV}\).

Solution:

  • Since TS is perpendicular to AND bisects WV, then TS is a diameter. This means that \(WU=VU\) and \(\bigtriangleup TUV\) is a right triangle.
  • Since we have a right triangle, we can use the Pythagorean Theorem to solve for \(\overline{TV}\).
  • We know that \(WU=4\), thus \(VU=4\). We also that \(TS=10\) and \(US=2\). We need the length of \(TU\). So, we can subtract \(US\) from \(TS\).
  • \(TU=TS-US \implies TU=10-2 \implies TU=8\)
  • Now, we can substitute the values of \(TU\) and \(UV\) into the Pythagorean Theorem to find the length of \(TV\).
  • \(TV^2=TU^2+UV^2 \implies TV^2=(8)^2+(4)^2 \implies TV^2=64+16 \implies TV^2=80 \implies TV=\sqrt{80}\).

The length of \(\overline{TV}\) is \(\sqrt{80}\).


Conclusion

Solving problems involving the measures of arcs, secants, tangents, and chords requires a deep understanding of their relationships. The Intersecting Chords Theorem, Two Secants Theorem, Secant-Tangent Theorem, and the special properties of tangents and radii provide the tools needed to solve for unknown lengths and angles in mathematical and real-world situations. By practicing these concepts, you are strengthening your ability to analyze geometric relationships in circles. Keep working with diagrams and equations to build confidence in solving these problems!
