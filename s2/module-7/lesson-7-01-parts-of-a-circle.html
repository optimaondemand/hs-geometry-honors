📚 VOCABULARY

Center: The point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is the center of the circle and whose sides are radii.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

Inscribed Angle: An angle whose vertex is on the circle and whose sides are chords of the circle.

Major Arc: The part of a circle's circumference that is larger than a semicircle.

Minor Arc: The part of a circle's circumference that is smaller than a semicircle.

Point of Tangency: The point at which a tangent line touches the circle.

Radius: A line segment from the center of the circle to any point on the circle.

Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.

Semicircle: Half of a circle, formed by a diameter dividing the circle into two equal arcs.

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point without crossing it.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the fascinating parts of a circle and the lines, segments, and angles associated with it. Circles are everywhere in the world around us, from the wheels on a car to the design of a clock. Understanding the parts of a circle is essential for developing your geometry skills and being able to describe how these components interact visually. During this lesson, you will learn how to identify the center, radius, diameter, chord, secant, tangent, and arcs of a circle, as well as angles such as central and inscribed angles. By the end of the lesson, you will be able to confidently recognize and label these parts in diagrams.


Parts of a Circle

A circle is defined as the set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a single point called the center. The radius is the distance from the center to any point on the circle. When two radii connect to form a straight line passing through the center, the resulting segment is called the diameter, which is the longest chord in a circle.

  • Circles are name by their center; .
  • The radius of the circle is .
  • The diameter of the circle is \(\overline{CD}\)



Segments in Circles

A chord is any line segment that has both of its endpoints on the circle, but unlike the diameter, it does not need to pass through the center. Sometimes, lines extend beyond the circle; a secant is a line that intersects the circle at two points, while a tangent is a line that touches the circle at exactly one point, called the point of tangency.

  • \(\overline{CB}\) is a Chord
  • \(\overline{DE}\) is a Secant
  • \(\overline{FG}\) is a Tangent and \(point\:A\) is the Point of Tangency



Angles in Circles

Circles also contain angles and arcs. A central angle is formed by two radii with its vertex at the center, while an inscribed angle is formed by two chords meeting at a point on the circle. The curved part of a circle’s circumference is called an arc. A minor arc is smaller than a semicircle, while a major arc is larger. When a diameter divides a circle into two equal arcs, each arc is a semicircle.

  • \(\angle CAB\) is a Central Angle
  • \(\angle CDE\) is an Inscribed Angle
  • \(\overset{\frown}{CB}\) is a Minor Arc (there are others)
  • \(\overset{\frown}{CDE}\) is a Major Arc (there are others)
  • \(\overset{\frown}{FG}\) is a Semicircle



Conclusion

Circles are more than just simple shapes; they are made up of a variety of components that work together to create interesting geometric relationships. By learning to identify the parts of a circle—its center, radius, diameter, chords, arcs, tangents, secants, and angles—you are building a foundation for more advanced topics in geometry. These parts are essential not only in math but also in understanding the structure of objects around us. As you continue your journey in geometry, remember how these basic elements connect and help us solve problems involving circles. Keep practicing with diagrams to strengthen your recognition skills!
