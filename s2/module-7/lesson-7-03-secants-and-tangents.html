📚 VOCABULARY

Center: The point inside a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A chord that passes through the center of the circle, making it the longest chord.

External Segment: The portion of a secant segment that lies outside the circle, extending from an external point to the circle's boundary.

Internal Segment: The portion of a secant segment that lies inside the circle, extending from the point where the secant enters the circle to the second intersection point.

Point of Tangency: The point at which a tangent line touches the circle.

Radius: A line segment from the center of the circle to any point on the circle.

Secant: A line that intersects a circle at two points.

Segment: A part of a line that has two endpoints and a measurable length. In a circle, segments can include chords, secant segments, radii, and diameters.

Tangent: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point without crossing it.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to solve problems involving the lengths of secants, tangents, and chords. These segments and lines are crucial components of circles, and understanding their relationships allows us to tackle both geometric and real-world problems. We will use key theorems, such as the Intersecting Chords Theorem, the Two Secants Theorem, and the Secant-Tangent Theorem, to find unknown lengths of these segments in given diagrams. By the end of this lesson, you will not only be able to calculate these lengths but also understand the mathematical relationships that connect them.


Properties, Postulates, and Theorems for Lines and Segments in Circles

Two Tangents Theorem

If two tangents are drawn from the same external point to a circle, the two tangent segments are always congruent.

  • \(\overline{PO}=\overline{NO}\)

Two Secants Theorem

If two secant segments are drawn from the same external point, the product of the external segment and the total length of one secant equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the other secant.

Formula

  • \(x(x+w)=y(y+z)\)

Secant-Tangent Theorem

If a tangent and a secant are drawn from the same external point, the square of the tangent’s length equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the secant.

Formula

  • \(g^2=f(f+e)\)



Example 1

In the given diagram, circle \(M\) has tangents \(PO\) and \(NO\).

\(PO=5x-4\) and \(NO=3x+6\).

Find the value of x and the length of the  \(\overline{PO}\) and \(\overline{NO}\).

Solution:

  • Since two tangents drawn from the same external point to a circle are always congruent, we can set up an equation to solve for \(x\).
  • \(PO=NO\)
  • We can substitute the expressions into the equation.
  • \(5x-4=3x+6 \implies 5x-3x-4=3x-3x+6 \implies 2x-4=6 \implies 2x-4+4=6+4 \implies 2x=10 \implies \frac{2x}{2}=\frac{10}{2} \implies x=5\)
  • Now that we know the value of x, we can substitute this into the expression for \(PO\) or \(NO\) to find the length of the tangents.
  • Since they are congruent, if we find one, we know both.
  • \(PO=5(5)-4 \implies PO=25-4 \implies PO=21\) which also means \(NO=21\).

The value of \(x\) is 5 and the length of \(\overline{PO}\) and \(\overline{NO}\) is 21 units.

 

Example 2

In the given diagram, circle \(G\) has secants \(\overline{KJ}\) and \(\overline{IJ}\) that intersect at point \(J\).

\(KL=2x+8\), \(LJ=4\), \(HJ=6\), and \(IH=3x-2\).

Find the value of x and find the length of segment \(KJ\).

Solution:

  • We can use the two secants theorem: If two secant segments are drawn from the same external point, the product of the external segment and the total length of one secant equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the other secant.
  • \(JL(JL+LK)=JH(JH+HI)\)
  • We can substitute the expressions into the equation.
  • We get the equation \(4(4+2x+8)=6(6+3x-2)\).
  • Now we can solve the equation for x.
  • \(4(2x+12)=6(3x+4) \implies 8x+48=18x+24 \implies 48-24=18x-8x \implies 24=10x \implies \frac{24}{10}=\frac{10x}{10} \implies x=2.4\).
  • Now that we know the value of x, we can substitute this in to find the value of \(KJ\). Notice that it is asking for the full length. So, we will use \(4+2x+8\) to find the length.
  • \(4+2(2.4)+8 \implies 4+4.8+8 \implies 16.8\)

The value of \(x\) is 2.4 and the length of segment \(KJ\) is 16.8.



Example 3

In the given diagram, circle \(M\) has secant \(\overline{OP}\) and tangent \(\overline{ON}\) that intersect at point \(O\).

\(OQ=4\), \(QP=8\), and \(ON=x\).

Find the length of \(\overline{ON}\).

Solution:

  • We can use the secant-tangent theorem: If a tangent and a secant are drawn from the same external point, the square of the tangent’s length equals the product of the external segment and the total length of the secant.
  • \(ON^2=OQ(OQ+QP)\)
  • We can substitute the values into the equation.
  • \(x^2=4(4+8) \implies x^2=4(12) \implies x^2=48 \implies x=\sqrt{48}\).
  • We can simplify the square root since \(48=3\cdot 16\) and 16 is a perfect square.
  • So, \(x=\sqrt{48}\) simplifies to \(x=4\sqrt{3}\). Either answer is acceptable.
  • ONLY convert to a rounded decimal if the question asks for an approximation.

The value of \(x\) is \(\sqrt{48}=4\sqrt{3}\).


Conclusion

Solving problems involving the measures of arcs, secants, tangents, and chords requires a deep understanding of their relationships. The Intersecting Chords Theorem, Two Secants Theorem, Secant-Tangent Theorem, and the special properties of tangents and radii provide the tools needed to solve for unknown lengths and angles in mathematical and real-world situations. By practicing these concepts, you are strengthening your ability to analyze geometric relationships in circles. Keep working with diagrams and equations to build confidence in solving these problems!
