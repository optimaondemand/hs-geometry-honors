📋 MODULE 11 VOCABULARY

Printable PDF Version

Base: The bottom surface of a solid figure that is typically used as a reference for measurement or slicing.

Center: The fixed point in a circle that is equidistant from every point on the circle.

Circle: A round two-dimensional shape where all points are equidistant from the center.

Cone: A three-dimensional figure with a circular base that tapers smoothly to a single point called the apex.

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by the intersection of a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis).

Cross-section: The two-dimensional shape created when a solid figure is sliced by a plane.

Cylinder: A three-dimensional figure with two parallel circular bases connected by a curved surface.

Diameter: A line segment that passes through the center of a circle and has endpoints on the circle; it is twice the length of the radius.

Distance Formula: A formula used to find the distance between two points in the coordinate plane \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Domain: The set of all possible x-values for which a function or relation is defined; for a circle, it represents the horizontal span of the graph.

Eccentricity: A measure of how much a conic section deviates from being circular; the eccentricity of a circle is always 0.

Equation: A mathematical statement that shows two expressions are equal.

Ellipse: A stretched circle, appearing as an oval; formed when a cone is sliced at an angle to its base.

Face: A flat surface that forms part of the boundary of a solid object.

Graph: A visual representation of mathematical relationships on the coordinate plane.

Horizontal: A direction parallel to the x-axis; side-to-side orientation.

Model: A representation of a real-world situation using mathematical concepts such as equations, diagrams, or graphs.

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane.

Parallel: Lines or planes that are always the same distance apart and never meet.

Perpendicular: Lines or planes that intersect at a 90-degree angle.

Plane: A flat surface that extends infinitely in all directions.

Point: An exact location in space, usually represented as a coordinate pair \((x,y)\) on the plane.

Polygon: A closed two-dimensional figure made up of straight line segments.

Prism: A solid figure with two parallel, congruent polygonal bases connected by rectangular faces.

Pyramid: A solid figure with a polygonal base and triangular faces that meet at a common point (the apex).

Quadrant: One of the four regions into which the coordinate plane is divided by the x- and y-axes.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle; half the length of the diameter.

Range: The set of all possible y-values for which a function or relation is defined; for a circle, it represents the vertical span of the graph.

Rectangle: A quadrilateral with four right angles and opposite sides that are equal in length.

Slice: A cut made through a solid figure, usually by a plane, to reveal a cross-section.

Solid: A three-dimensional geometric figure with length, width, and height.

Square: A quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles.

Standard Form (of a Circle's Equation): The equation \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\), where \((h,k)\) is the center and \(r\) is the radius.

Symmetry: A balanced and proportional similarity; a circle has symmetry about its center, x-axis, and y-axis.

Translation: A type of transformation that slides a figure a certain distance in a given direction on the coordinate plane.

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

Three-Dimensional: A shape or figure that has depth in addition to length and width.

Two-Dimensional: A flat shape or figure that has only length and width.

Vertical: Positioned upright or perpendicular to the horizon or base.

x-coordinate: The horizontal value in a pair of coordinates; it tells how far left or right the point is from the origin.

y-coordinate: The vertical value in a pair of coordinates; it tells how far up or down the point is from the origin.
