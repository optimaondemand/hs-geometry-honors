📚 VOCABULARY

Center: The fixed point from which all points on a circle are equidistant.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are the same distance from a given point called the center.

Coordinate Plane: A flat, two-dimensional surface defined by a horizontal axis (x-axis) and a vertical axis (y-axis).

Domain: The set of all possible x-values for which a function or relation is defined; for a circle, it represents the horizontal span of the graph.

Eccentricity: A measure of how much a conic section deviates from being circular; the eccentricity of a circle is always 0.

Equation: A mathematical statement showing that two expressions are equal.

Graph: A visual representation of a mathematical relationship using points on a coordinate plane.

Horizontal: A direction parallel to the x-axis; side-to-side orientation.

Origin: The point (0, 0) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect.

Point: A location in space with no size or shape, represented by coordinates (x, y) on the plane.

Quadrant: One of the four regions into which the coordinate plane is divided by the x- and y-axes.

Radius: The distance from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Range: The set of all possible y-values for which a function or relation is defined; for a circle, it represents the vertical span of the graph.

Standard Form (of a Circle's Equation): The equation \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\), where \((h,k)\) is the center and \(r\) is the radius.

Symmetry: A balanced and proportional similarity; a circle has symmetry about its center, x-axis, and y-axis.

Translation: A transformation that moves a figure without rotating or changing its size or shape.

x-coordinate: The horizontal value in a coordinate pair that tells how far a point is from the y-axis.

y-coordinate: The vertical value in a coordinate pair that tells how far a point is from the x-axis.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to graph circles using their equations and interpret their key features: center, radius, domain, range, and eccentricity. Understanding these features allows us to describe, analyze, and create accurate graphs of circles in the coordinate plane. We'll also learn how changes to the equation of a circle affect its position and size, and how this transformation connects to the concept of translation in geometry.


Key Features of a Circle

A circle is defined as the set of all points in a plane that are a fixed distance from a given point called the center. This distance is called the radius. The equation of a circle in standard form is \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\).

From this equation, we can identify:

  • Center: The values of \(h\) and \(k\) for the center \((h,k)\).
  • Radius: The square root of the number the equation equals, \(r\).
  • Domain: The horizontal span of the circle; \(D=\{x|h-r\leq x\leq h+r\}\)
  • Range: The vertical span of the circle, \(R=\{y|k-r\leq y\leq k+r\}\)
  • Eccentricity: A circle's eccentricity is always 0, meaning it is perfectly round.



Example 1

The equation representing a circle is \((x+2)^2+(y-5)^2=49\).

Identify the center, radius, domain, range, and eccentricity of the circle.

Solution:

  • We can begin by identifying the center of the circle as \((-2,5)\).
  • Next, we can identify the radius by taking the square root of 49; \(r=7\).
  • Now we will work on finding the domain. We begin by subtracting the radius from the x value of the center. Then, adding the radius to the x value of the center.
    • \(-2-7=-9\) for the left side of the horizontal span for the domain.
    • \(-2+7=5\) for the right side of the horizontal span for the domain.
  • Domain: \(-9\leq x\leq 5\)
  • We will work the range in a similar fashion, except we use the y value of the center this time.
    • \(5-7=-2\) for the bottom part of the vertical span.
    • \(5+7=12\) for the top part of the vertical span.
  • Range: \(-2\leq y\leq 12\)
  • For the eccentricity, since it is a perfectly round circle, \(e=0\).

Key Features:

Center: \((-2,5)\)

Radius: \(7\)

Domain: \(-9\leq x\leq 5\)

Range: \(-2\leq y\leq 12\)

Eccentricity: \(e=0\)



When we identify key features of a circle, it can help us understand how to create the graph of the circle. Now we will explore graphing equations of circles.

Graphing a Circle from the Equation

To graph a circle:

  1. Identify the center \((h,k)\).

  2. Use the radius \(r\) to plot points up, down, left, and right from the center.

  3. Using the circle tool, create a round curve that passes through those points and is symmetric about the center.



Example 2

Graph the circle that represents the equation \((x-1)^2+(y+2)^2=36\). Then identify the key features of the circle.

Solution:

  • We begin by identifying the center of the circle; the h value is 1 and the k value is -2.
  • The center is \((1,-2)\).
  • Next, we identify the radius; we take the square root of 36.
  • The radius is \(r=6\).
  • Now we can use this information to plot points. We plot the center first. Then we plot at least one other point 6 units either up, down, left, or right from the center.
  • Finally, use the circle tool to draw the circle. Start at the center, then draw the circle out to the second point that is 6 units away from the center.
  • Now, looking at the graph, we can identify the key features. To graph the circle, we have already identified the center and the radius.
  • We can next identify the domain. We see that the horizontal span of the circle starts at -5 on the left and spans to 7 on the right of the circle.
  • Domain: \(\{-5\leq x\leq 7\}\)
  • Now we can look at the vertical span of the circle. The bottom of the circle is at -8 and the top of the circle is at 4.
  • Range: \(\{-8\leq y\leq 4\}\)
  • Finally, we identify the eccentricity. Since it is a perfectly round circle, \(e=0\).

Key Features:

  • Center: \((1,-2)\)
  • Radius: \(5\)
  • Domain: \(\{-5\leq x\leq 7\}\)
  • Range: \(\{-8\leq y\leq 4\}\)
  • Eccentricity: \(e=0\)



Example 3

A circle has a center of \((-4,-6)\) and a point on the circle is \((-2,-1)\).

Create a graph to represent the circle, write the equation of the circle, and identify the key features of the circle.

Solution:

  • First, we will plot the center and the point. Then use the circle tool starting at the center and drawing a circle out to the point on the circle.
  • Now, we need the center and the radius to write the equation of the circle.
  • We already know the center is \((-4,-6)\).
  • We need to find the radius, which we can use the center and the point on the circle in the distance formula.
    • \((-4,-6)=(x_1,y_1)\) and \((-2,-1)=(x_2,y_2)\)
    • \(r=\sqrt{(-2+4)^2+(-1+6)^2} \implies r=\sqrt{(2)^2+(5)^2} \implies r=\sqrt{4+25} \implies r=\sqrt{29}\)
  • Now that we have the center and the radius, we can write the equation of the circle: \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\)
    • \(h=-4\), \(k=-6\), and \(r=\sqrt{29}\)
    • Equation: \((x+4)^2+(y+6)^2=29\)
  • Last, we will identify all the key features.
  • We already have the center and the radius. So, we will use these to find the domain and range.
  • To find the domain, we subtract and add the radius to the x value of the center. In this example, we cannot use the graph since the left side and right side of the circle are not on an exact number.
    • Left Side: \(-4-\sqrt{29}\) - since we cannot subtract the square root from -4, we will leave that as such.
    • Right Side: \(-4+\sqrt{29}\)
    • Domain: \(\{-4-\sqrt{29} \leq x\leq -4+\sqrt{29}\}\)
  • We will do the same for the range, but with the y value of the center.
    • Bottom: \(-6-\sqrt{29}\)
    • Top: \(-6+\sqrt{29}\)
    • Range: \(\{-6-\sqrt{29} \leq y\leq -6+\sqrt{29}\}\)
  • Finally, since the circle is perfectly round the eccentricity is \(e=0\).

Key Features:

  • Center: \((-4,-6)\)
  • Radius: \(\sqrt{29}\)
  • Domain: \(\{-4-\sqrt{29} \leq x\leq -4+\sqrt{29}\}\)
  • Range: \(\{-6-\sqrt{29} \leq y\leq -6+\sqrt{29}\}\)
  • Eccentricity: \(e=0\)



Conclusion

Understanding how to read, write, and graph the standard form of a circle’s equation is a foundational skill in geometry. By identifying the center, radius, domain, and range, we can accurately graph a circle and describe its key properties. The concept of eccentricity confirms that a circle is a perfect shape among conic sections. As we change the values of hhh, kkk, and rrr, we translate and resize the circle without distorting its round shape. This understanding lays the groundwork for future lessons in solving and applying circle equations in mathematical and real-world contexts.
