📚 VOCABULARY

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis) that intersect at the origin.

Coordinates: An ordered pair of numbers \((x,y)\) used to describe the position of a point on the coordinate plane.

Distance Formula: A formula used to calculate the distance between two points on the coordinate plane: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Diagonal: A line segment that connects two non-adjacent vertices of a polygon.

Midpoint Formula: A formula used to find the point exactly halfway between two given points: \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis meet on the coordinate plane.

Parallelogram: A quadrilateral with two pairs of opposite sides that are parallel and congruent.

Perpendicular Lines: Two lines that intersect at a right angle (90 degrees).

Quadrilateral: A polygon with exactly four sides and four angles.

Rectangle: A parallelogram with four right angles and opposite sides that are congruent.

Rhombus: A parallelogram with four sides of equal length and diagonals that are perpendicular to each other.

Right Angle: An angle that measures exactly 90 degrees.

Side Length: The measurement of the distance between two connected vertices of a polygon.

Slope: A number that describes the steepness of a line, calculated as the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.

Slope Formula: A formula used to find the slope between two points: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).​

Square: A quadrilateral that is both a rectangle and a rhombus; it has four sides of equal length and four right angles.

Trapezoid: A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of opposite sides that are parallel.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to classify a quadrilateral presented on the coordinate plane using the slope formula and distance formula. Quadrilaterals can be identified by their side lengths, parallel sides, and right angles. After learning how to classify a quadrilateral based on these properties, we will also learn how to find the fourth vertex of a quadrilateral when given the type of quadrilateral and three of its vertices. Both slope and distance will help us ensure the correct relationships between the sides and angles to complete and identify quadrilaterals accurately.


Properties of Special Quadrilaterals

  • Parallelogram: Opposite sides are parallel and congruent.

  • Rectangle: All angles are right angles (perpendicular adjacent sides) and opposite sides are congruent.

  • Rhombus: All sides are congruent and parallel.

  • Square: All sides are congruent and all angles are right angles.


Classifying a Quadrilateral Using Slope and Distance

A quadrilateral is a polygon with four sides. Different types of quadrilaterals can be classified by using:

  • Distance Formula to measure side lengths.

  • Slope Formula to check for parallel or perpendicular sides.

Steps to Classify a Quadrilateral:

  • Find the slope of each side using the Slope Formula: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)​

    • Equal slopes → sides are parallel.

    • Negative reciprocal slopes → sides are perpendicular (right angles).

  • Find the length of each side using the Distance Formula: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\)

    • Equal lengths → congruent sides.

  • Based on the side lengths and slopes, classify the quadrilateral:

    • Parallelogram
    • Rectangle
    • Rhombus
    • Square



Example 1

A quadrilateral has vertices at \(A(-4,4)\), \(B(2,6)\), \(C(4,0)\), and \(D(-2,-2)\). Classify what type of quadrilateral the coordinates form.

Solution:

  • We should begin with graphing the coordinates to understand the sides and which ones are opposite.
  • Now, we will use the slope formula to determine parallel sides and check for perpendicular sides.
    • Parallel sides will confirm it is a parallelogram
    • Perpendicular sides will confirm right angles or not.
    • Slope of AB - \(A(-4,4)\) and \(B(2,6)\)
      • \(m=\frac{6-4}{2+4}=\frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}\)
    • Slope of BC - \(B(2,6)\) and \(C(4,0)\)
      • \(m=\frac{0-6}{4-2}=\frac{-6}{2}=\frac{-3}{1}\)
    • Slope of CD - \(C(4,0)\) and \(D(-2,-2)\) 
      • \(m=\frac{-2-0}{-2-4}=\frac{-2}{-6}=\frac{1}{3}\)
    • Slope of AD - \(A(-4,4)\) and \(D(-2,-2)\)
      • \(m=\frac{-2-4}{-2+4}=\frac{-6}{2}=\frac{-3}{1}\)
    • We see that opposite sides are parallel since AB and CD have the same slope \(\frac{1}{3}\), and BC and AD have the same slope \(\frac{-3}{1}\).
    • We also see that adjacent sides are perpendicular since AB and BC have negative reciprocal slopes \(\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{-3}{1}\), and BC and CD have negative reciprocal slopes \(\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{-3}{1}\).
  • This allows one to see that the quadrilateral is either a rectangle or a square since the perpendicular sides show the quadrilateral has right angles.
  • Next, we will use the distance formula to find the lengths of all sides to determine if it is a rectangle or a square.
    • Length of AB - \(A(-4,4)\) and \(B(2,6)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(2+4)^2+(6-4)^2}=\sqrt{40}\)
    • Length of BC - \(B(2,6)\) and \(C(4,0)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(4-2)^2+(6-0)^2}=\sqrt{40}\)
    • Length of CD - \(C(4,0)\) and \(D(-2,-2)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(-2-4)^2+(-2-0)^2}=\sqrt{40}\)
    • Length of AD - \(A(-4,4)\) and \(D(-2,-2)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(-2+4)^2+(-2-4)^2}=\sqrt{40}\)
    • We see that all sides are congruent.

We can classify this quadrilateral as a square since opposite sides are parallel, adjacent sides are perpendicular forming right angles, and all sides are congruent.



Example 2

A quadrilateral has vertices at \(A(-5,3)\), \(B(-1,7)\), \(C(3,5)\), and \(D(-1,1)\). Classify what type of quadrilateral the coordinates form.

Solution:

  • We should begin with graphing the coordinates to understand the sides and which ones are opposite.
  • Now, we will use the slope formula to determine parallel sides and check for perpendicular sides.
    • Parallel sides will confirm it is a parallelogram
    • Perpendicular sides will confirm right angles or not.
    • Slope of AB - \(A(-5,3)\) and \(B(-1,7)\)
      • \(m=\frac{7-3}{-1+5}=\frac{4}{4}=\frac{1}{1}\)
    • Slope of BC - \(B(-1,7)\) and \(C(3,5)\)
      • \(m=\frac{5-7}{3+1}=\frac{-2}{4}=\frac{-1}{2}\)
    • Slope of CD - \(C(3,5)\) and \(D(-1,1)\) 
      • \(m=\frac{1-5}{-1-3}=\frac{-4}{-4}=\frac{1}{1}\)
    • Slope of AD - \(A(-5,3)\) and \(D(-1,1)\)
      • \(m=\frac{1-3}{-1+5}=\frac{-2}{4}=\frac{-1}{2}\)
    • We see that opposite sides are parallel since AB and CD have the same slope \(\frac{1}{1}\), and BC and AD have the same slope \(\frac{-1}{2}\).
    • We also see that adjacent sides are NOT perpendicular since none of them have negative reciprocal slopes.
  • This allows one to see that the quadrilateral is either a parallelogram or a rhombus since the sides are only parallel.
  • Next, we will use the distance formula to find the lengths of all sides to determine if it is a parallelogram or rhombus.
    • Length of AB - \(A(-5,3)\) and \(B(-1,7)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(-1+5)^2+(7-3)^2}=\sqrt{32}\)
    • Length of BC - \(B(-1,7)\) and \(C(3,5)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(3+1)^2+(5-7)^2}=\sqrt{20}\)
    • Length of CD - \(C(3,5)\) and \(D(-1,1)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(-1-3)^2+(1-5)^2}=\sqrt{32}\)
    • Length of AD - \(A(-5,3)\) and \(D(-1,1)\)
      • \(d=\sqrt{(-1+5)^2+(1-3)^2}=\sqrt{20}\)
    • We see that opposite sides are congruent.
      • AB and CD have a length of \(\sqrt{32}\).
      • BC and AD have a length of \(sqrt{20}\).

We can classify this quadrilateral as a parallelogram since opposite sides are parallel and opposite sides are congruent.



Finding the Fourth Vertex of a Quadrilateral

Sometimes you are given three vertices of a quadrilateral and asked to find the fourth vertex to create a specific type of quadrilateral (like a parallelogram, rectangle, or square).

Steps to Find the Fourth Vertex:

  1. Use the properties of the quadrilateral to set up what you know.

  2. Match parallel sides (equal slopes) or perpendicular sides to form right angles (negative reciprocal slopes).

  3. Solve for the missing coordinates based on what needs to be true.

Important Clues:

  • In a parallelogram (and rhombus), opposite sides must be parallel (equal slopes).

  • In a rectangle (and square), adjacent sides must be perpendicular (slopes are negative reciprocals).



Example 3

A parallelogram has three vertices \(A(-6,3)\), \(B(3,5)\), and \(C(1,-3)\).

Find the fourth vertex, D, to complete the parallelogram.

Solution:

  • We can begin by graphing the three vertices we know and completing two sides of the parallelogram.
  • Next, using the graph, we will count the vertical distance from B to C and then the horizontal distance from B to C. This will allow us to replicate this from A to find the location of D.
  • Using these measures, we are ensuring the sides BC and AD are parallel as the change in vertical distance over change in horizontal is the slope.
  • This also ensures that the lengths of BC and AD are equal since distance is the Pythagorean Theorem on the coordinate plane, which uses the lengths of the legs of the right triangle formed to find the diagonal distance.
  • We count the vertical distance from B down to C as 8, then over to C as 2 for the horizontal distance. We go to point A and replicate this to find the location of point D.
  • Then we connect C to D and A to D to complete the parallelogram.
  • We find that the coordinates of the fourth vertex of Parallelogram ABCD is \(D(-8,-5)\).



Conclusion

Using slope and distance formulas allows us to classify quadrilaterals on the coordinate plane by checking for parallel sides, perpendicular sides, and congruent side lengths. Knowing these properties also helps us find a missing vertex when given three points and a quadrilateral type. By following the methods step-by-step, you can confidently determine both the classification and the complete shape of any quadrilateral given in coordinate geometry!
