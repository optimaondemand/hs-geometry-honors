📚 VOCABULARY

Center of a Circle: The fixed point in a circle that is the same distance from every point on the circle.

Circle: The set of all points in a plane that are a fixed distance from a given point called the center.

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by two perpendicular number lines (the x-axis and y-axis) that intersect at the origin.

Coordinates: An ordered pair of numbers \((x,y)\) that represent the position of a point on the coordinate plane.

Distance Formula: A formula used to calculate the distance between two points in a plane: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Midpoint Formula: A formula used to find the point halfway between two given points: \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane.

Point-Slope Formula: A formula used to write the equation of a line when given a point and a slope: \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\).

Radius: The distance from the center of a circle to any point on the circle.

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, found by the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change between two points.

Slope Formula: A formula used to find the slope between two points: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).

Tangent Line: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point and is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to solve problems involving circles on the coordinate plane. First, you will learn how to verify if a given point lies on a circle using the center and radius. Then, you will learn how to find the center of a circle when given the endpoints of a diameter, even when the diameter is not parallel to the x-axis or y-axis. Finally, you will learn how to write the equation of a line that is tangent to a circle at a given point of tangency using slope and point-slope form. This lesson will build your understanding of circles without needing the full equation of a circle.


Verifying if a Point is on a Circle

To verify if a point lies on a circle:

  1. Find the distance between the point and the center of the circle using the Distance Formula.

  2. Compare the distance to the radius:

    • If the distance equals the radius, the point lies on the circle.

    • If not, the point is either inside or outside the circle.



Example 1

Given a circle with center \((3,4)\) and a radius of 5, which point lies on the circle?

  1. \((-2,6)\)
  2. \((8,5)\)
  3. \((2,9)\)
  4. \((7,1)\)

Solution:

  • To find which point lies on the circle, we will find the distance between the center and each point.
  • The one that has a distance that is equal to the radius of the circle lies on the circle.
  • We will begin with finding the distance between option A and the center.
    • \((3,4)\) and \((-2,6)\)
    • \(d=\sqrt{(-2-3)^2+(6-4)^2} \implies d=\sqrt{29} \implies d\approx 5.385...\)
    • The distance is not equal to the radius 5. Therefore the point \((-2,6)\) in option A does not lie on the circle.
  • We will begin with finding the distance between option B and the center.
    • \((3,4)\) and \((8,5)\)
    • \(d=\sqrt{(8-3)^2+(5-4)^2} \implies d=\sqrt{26} \implies d\approx 5.099...\)
    • The distance is not equal to the radius 5. Therefore the point \((8,5)\) in option B does not lie on the circle.
  • We will begin with finding the distance between option C and the center.
    • \((3,4)\) and \((2,9)\)
    • \(d=\sqrt{(2-3)^2+(9-4)^2} \implies d=\sqrt{26} \implies d\approx 5.099...\)
    • The distance is not equal to the radius 5. Therefore the point \((2,9)\) in option C does not lie on the circle.
  • We will begin with finding the distance between option D and the center.
    • \((3,4)\) and \((7,1)\)
    • \(d=\sqrt{(7-3)^2+(1-4)^2} \implies d=\sqrt{25} \implies d=5\)
    • The distance IS equal to the radius 5. Therefore the point \((7,1)\) in option D DOES lie on the circle.

We find that point \((7,1)\) lies on the circle since the distance between it and the center is 5, which is equal to the radius 5.



Finding the Center of a Circle with a Given Diameter

When given the endpoints of a diameter, the center is the midpoint of those two points.

Use the Midpoint Formula 

\((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\)

where \((x_1,y_1)\) represents one end of the diameter and \((x_2,y_2)\) represents the other endpoint of the diameter.



Example 2

A circle where \(\overline{AB}\) is the diameter with endpoints at \((-3,-1)\) and \((8,3)\) as shown.

Find the coordinates of the center of the circle.

Solution:

  • To find the coordinates of the center of the circle, we need to find the midpoint of the diameter.
  • We will use the coordinates of the endpoints of the diameter \((-3,-1)\) and \((8,3)\).
  • \((\frac{-3+8}{2},\frac{-1+3}{2})\implies (\frac{5}{2},\frac{2}{2})\implies (2.5,1)\)

We find that the coordinates of the center of the circle are \((2.5,1)\).



Writing the Equation of a Tangent Line to a Circle

To write the equation of a tangent line:

  1. Find the slope of the radius connecting the center to the point of tangency using the Slope Formula \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).
    • This step gives the direction of the radius. Since the tangent touches the circle at exactly one point, it must be perpendicular to the radius at that point.
  2. Find the negative reciprocal of the radius's slope to get the slope of the tangent line.
    • Tangent lines are always perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals of each other.
  3. Use the Point-Slope Formula \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\), where \((x_1,y_1)\) is the point of tangency and \(m\) is the slope of the tangent line.
    • This form allows you to easily plug in a point and slope to write the equation of the line that just "grazes" the circle.



Example 3

Find the equation of the line tangent to the given circle with a point of tangency at point B.

Solution:

  • We begin by finding the slope of the radius using the center and the point of tangency.
    • \((-2,2)\) and \((3,4)\)
    • \(slope=\frac{4-2}{3+2} \implies slope=\frac{2}{5}\)
  • We will now find the negative reciprocal of the slope we find which will represent the slope of our tangent lines.
    • Tangent lines are perpendicular to the circle and perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes.
    • Tangent Line Slope: \(m=-\frac{5}{2}\)
  • Last, we use point-slope form with the slope we just found in step 2 and the point of tangency \((3,4)\) to write the equation.
    • Point-Slope Form: \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\) where \(m=-\frac{5}{2}\) and \((x_1,y_1)=(3,4)\).
    • Equation of Tangent Line: \(y-4=-\frac{5}{2}(x-3)\)

We find that the equation of the tangent line to circle at point \((3,4)\) is \(y-4=-\frac{5}{2}(x-3)\).



Conclusion

Today you learned how to solve several types of problems involving circles on the coordinate plane. You learned to verify if a point lies on a circle by using the center and radius and the Distance Formula. You also found the center of a circle when given a diameter by applying the Midpoint Formula, even when the diameter was not horizontal or vertical. Finally, you learned how to write the equation of a line tangent to a circle by finding the slope of the radius and using its negative reciprocal in the Point-Slope Formula. With practice, you will be able to tackle many coordinate plane problems involving circles with confidence!
