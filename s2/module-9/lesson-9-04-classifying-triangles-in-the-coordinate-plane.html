Classifying Triangles in the Coordinate Plane
Lesson
📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Triangle: A triangle with all three angles measuring less than 90 degrees.

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis) that intersect at the origin.

Coordinates: A pair of numbers \((x,y)\) that locate a point on the coordinate plane.

Distance Formula: A formula used to calculate the distance between two points on the coordinate plane: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Equilateral Triangle: A triangle with all three sides equal in length and all three angles equal in measure.

Hypotenuse: The side opposite the right angle in a right triangle; it is the longest side.

Isosceles Triangle: A triangle with at least two sides of equal length.

Obtuse Triangle: A triangle with one angle greater than 90 degrees.

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane.

Right Triangle: A triangle that has one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees.

Scalene Triangle: A triangle with no sides of equal length.

Side Length: The distance between two endpoints that form one side of a triangle.

Slope: A measure of the steepness of a line, calculated as the ratio of the vertical change to the horizontal change between two points.

Slope Formula: A formula used to find the slope between two points: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\)​

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to classify triangles based on their sides and angles, and how to use coordinate geometry tools like the Distance Formula, Slope Formula, and Pythagorean Theorem to classify triangles accurately. Triangles can be sorted by their side lengths into scalene, isosceles, and equilateral triangles, or by their angle measures into acute, right, and obtuse triangles. We will learn how to calculate side lengths and slopes on the coordinate plane and use those results to determine the type of triangle.


Using the Distance Formula to Classify Triangles by Side Lengths

To classify a triangle by its sides, first find the length of each side using the Distance Formula: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Steps:

  1. Label the vertices of the triangle (for example, A, B, and C).

  2. Use the Distance Formula to find the length of each side: \(AB\), \(BC\), and \(AC\).

  3. Compare the side lengths:

    • If all sides are different, it’s a scalene triangle.

    • If two sides are equal, it’s an isosceles triangle.

    • If all three sides are equal, it’s an equilateral triangle.


Types of Triangles by Side Lengths

Scalene Triangle

All sides are different lengths.

\(A(-4,-1)\), \(B(-2,5)\), \(C(7,3)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(-2+4)^2+(5+1)^2}=\sqrt{40}\approx 6.325\)

\(BC=\sqrt{(3-5)^2+(7+2)^2}=\sqrt{85}\approx 9.220\)

\(CA=\sqrt{(7+4)^2+(3+1)^2}=\sqrt{137}\approx 11.705\)

Isosceles Triangle

Two sides are equal in length.

\(A(-4,-1)\), \(B(-2,5)\), \(C(4,3)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(-2+4)^2+(5+1)^2}=\sqrt{40}\approx 6.325\)

\(BC=\sqrt{(4+2)^2+(3-5)^2}=\sqrt{40}\approx 6.325\)

\(CA=\sqrt{(4+4)^2+(3+1)^2}=\sqrt{80}\approx 8.944\)

Equilateral Triangle

All three sides are equal in length.

\(A(-1.5,-5.5)\), \(B(4,0)\), \(C(-3.5,2)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(4+1.5)^2+(0+5.5)^2}=\sqrt{60.5}\approx 7.778\)

\(BC=\sqrt{(-3.5-4)^2+(2-0)^2}=\sqrt{60.25}\approx 7.762\)

\(CA=\sqrt{(-3.5-1.5)^2+(2+5.5)^2}=\sqrt{60.25}\approx 7.762\)

*Note that the coordinates of C are an approximation as it is not exactly at \(C(-3.5,2)\). So, the distances between the points are approximations. They are close enough to say it is likely an equilateral triangle.




Using the Slope Formula to Show Perpendicular Line Segments and Classify as Right

To check if a triangle is a right triangle, find the slope of each side using the Slope Formula: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\).

Steps:

  1. Find the slope of each side.

  2. Check for perpendicular slopes: If two slopes are negative reciprocals (for example, \(2\) and \(-\frac{1}{2}\) ), the two sides are perpendicular, meaning they form a right angle.

  3. If there is a right angle, classify the triangle as a right triangle.


Using the Pythagorean Theorem and Side Lengths to Classify as Right or Obtuse

After finding the side lengths, you can also use the Pythagorean Theorem to classify the triangle:

1. Acute Triangle: \(a^2+b^2>c^2\)

2. Obtuse Triangle: \(a^2+b^2<c^2\)

Steps:

  1. Identify the longest side (hypotenuse candidate).

  2. Square the side lengths.

  3. Compare \(a^2+b^2\) with \(c^2\) to classify.


Types of Triangles by Angle Measures

Acute Triangle

All angles are less than 90 degrees.

\(A(-5,-2)\), \(B(-1,4)\), \(C(2.5,1)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(-1+5)^2+(4+2)^2}=\sqrt{52}\)

\(BC=\sqrt{(2.5+1)^2+(1-4)^2}=\sqrt{21.25}\)

\(CA=\sqrt{(2.5+5)^2+(1+2)^2}=\sqrt{65.25}\)

\(a^2+b^2=\sqrt{52}^2+\sqrt{21.25}^2=52+21.25=73.25\)

\(c^2=\sqrt{65.25}^2=65.25\)

Since \(73.25>65.25\), the triangle is acute.

Obtuse Triangle

One angle is greater than 90 degrees.

\(A(-3,-3)\), \(B(-1,4)\), \(C(6,3.5)\)

\(AB=\sqrt{(-1+3)^2+(4+3)^2}=\sqrt{53}\)

\(BC=\sqrt{(6+1)^2+(3.5-4)^2}=\sqrt{49.25}\)

\(CA=\sqrt{(6+3)^2+(3.5+3)^2}=\sqrt{123.25}\)

\(a^2+b^2=\sqrt{53}^2+\sqrt{49.25}^2=53+49.25=102.25\)

\(c^2=\sqrt{123.25}^2=123.25\)

Since \(102.25<123.25\), the triangle is obtuse.

Right Triangle

One angle measures exactly 90 degrees.

Right angles are formed by perpendicular lines. Perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes.

It appears that \(\angle ABC\) might be a right angle. So, we can check if \(AB\) and \(BC\) are perpendicular.

Slope of AB: \(m=\frac{4+2}{-1+3}=\frac{6}{2}=\frac{3}{1}\)

Slope of BC: \(m=\frac{3-4}{2+1}=\frac{-1}{3}\)

Since the slopes of the lines that appear to form a right angle are negative reciprocals of each other, then \(\angle ABC\) is a right angle.

This makes \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) a right triangle.



Conclusion

By understanding how triangles are classified by their sides and angles, and by using coordinate geometry tools like the Distance Formula, Slope Formula, and Pythagorean Theorem, you can accurately determine the type of any triangle drawn on the coordinate plane. Using side lengths tells us about scalene, isosceles, or equilateral triangles, while using slopes and side lengths together allows us to tell if a triangle is acute, right, or obtuse. With practice, these methods will become quick and natural whenever you analyze triangles in Geometry!
