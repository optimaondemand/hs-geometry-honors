📚 VOCABULARY

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis) that intersect at the origin.

Coordinates: A pair of numbers \((x,y)\) that indicate the location of a point on the coordinate plane.

Endpoint: A point marking the end of a line segment.

Fraction: A number that shows part of a whole, written with a numerator and denominator.

Horizontal Distance: The distance measured along the x-axis between two points.

Line Segment: A part of a line that is bounded by two endpoints.

Midpoint: The point that is exactly halfway between two endpoints of a line segment.

Midpoint Formula: A formula used to find the midpoint between two points: \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane.

Partition: To divide a line segment into parts that correspond to a given ratio.

Ratio: A comparison of two quantities using division.

Section Formula: A formula used to find the point that partitions a line segment into a given ratio: \((\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n})\).

Vertical Distance: The distance measured along the y-axis between two points.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore what it means to partition a line segment and how to do it mathematically. Partitioning a line segment means dividing it into two parts that match a given ratio. Using coordinate geometry, we can find the exact point that separates the segment into the correct parts. To do this, we apply a formula called the Section Formula, which calculates the coordinates of the partition point based on the ratio and the coordinates of the endpoints. Throughout this lesson, we will focus on mathematical examples to understand how to partition a line segment accurately.


What Does It Mean to Partition a Line Segment?

To partition a line segment means to split it into two parts that are proportional to a given ratio. For example, if a segment is divided in a 2:3 ratio, the first part is 2 parts long and the second part is 3 parts long.


How to Partition a Line Segment Given a Ratio and Endpoints:

When given endpoints \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\) and a ratio \(m:n\), use the Section Formula:

\(\Large (\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n})\)


Steps:

  1. Multiply the coordinates of the second endpoint by the first part of the ratio.

  2. Multiply the coordinates of the first endpoint by the second part of the ratio.

  3. Add the results together.

  4. Divide by the sum of the parts of the ratio.



Example 1

Find the point that partitions the line segment with endpoints \((2,4)\) and \((8,10)\) in a \(1:2\) ratio.

Solution:

  • First Part = AC = 1 and Second Part = CB = 2
  • Name Endpoints - \((x_1,y_1)=(2,4)\) and \((x_2,y_2)=(8,10)\)
  • Ration - \(m:n=1:2\)
  • Substitute into Section Formula
    • \(\Large (\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{1\cdot 8+2\cdot 2}{1+2},\frac{1\cdot 10+2\cdot 4}{1+2})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{8+4}{3},\frac{10+8}{3})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{12}{3},\frac{18}{3})\)
    • \((4,6)\)

The partition point is \((4,6)\).



Example 2

Find the point that partitions the line segment with endpoints \((-3,1)\) and \((6,7)\) in a \(2:5\) ratio.

Solution:

  • First Part = AC = 2 and Second Part = CB = 5
  • Name Endpoints - \((x_1,y_1)=(-3,1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)=(6,7)\)
  • Ration - \(m:n=2:5\)
  • Substitute into Section Formula
    • \(\Large (\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{2\cdot 6+5\cdot -3}{2+5},\frac{2\cdot 7+5\cdot 1}{2+5})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{12+-15}{7},\frac{14+5}{7})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{-3}{7},\frac{19}{7})\)
    • Since dividing by 7 would create a repeating decimal, we will leave the answer in fraction form.

The partition point is \((\frac{-3}{7},\frac{19}{7})\).



Example 3

Find the point that partitions the line segment with endpoints \((1.5,2)\) and \((7,5.5)\) in a \(3:2\) ratio.

Solution:

  • First Part = AC = 3 and Second Part = CB = 2
  • Name Endpoints - \((x_1,y_1)=(1.5,2)\) and \((x_2,y_2)=(7,5.5)\)
  • Ration - \(m:n=3:2\)
  • Substitute into Section Formula
    • \(\Large (\frac{mx_2+nx_1}{m+n},\frac{my_2+ny_1}{m+n})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{3\cdot 7+2\cdot 1.5}{3+2},\frac{3\cdot 5.5+2\cdot 2}{3+2})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{21+3}{5},\frac{16.5+4}{5})\)
    • \(\Large (\frac{24}{5},\frac{20.5}{5})\)
    • \((4.8,4.1)\)

The partition point is \((4.8,4.1)\).



Conclusion

Partitioning a line segment means dividing it into parts according to a given ratio. By using the Section Formula, you can find the exact coordinates of the partition point whether you are working with whole numbers or decimals. Mastering this method allows you to accurately divide segments in Geometry and apply proportional reasoning to more advanced problems. Practice applying the formula step-by-step to become confident when partitioning line segments!
