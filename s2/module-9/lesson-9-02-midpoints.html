📚 VOCABULARY

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by a horizontal number line (x-axis) and a vertical number line (y-axis) that intersect at a point called the origin.

Coordinates: A pair of numbers \((x,y)\) that indicate the position of a point on the coordinate plane.

Endpoint: A point at one end of a line segment.

Fraction: A number that represents a part of a whole, written as one number over another, separated by a line.

Horizontal Distance: The distance measured along the x-axis between two points.

Line Segment: A part of a line that has two endpoints and includes all points between them.

Midpoint: The point that is exactly halfway between two endpoints of a line segment.

Midpoint Formula: A formula used to find the midpoint of a line segment given two endpoints; \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

Origin: The point where the x-axis and y-axis intersect, represented by the coordinates \((0,0)\).

Partition: To divide a line segment into parts, often based on a specific ratio.

Ratio: A comparison of two numbers by division, showing how many times one value contains or is contained within the other.

Vertical Distance: The distance measured along the y-axis between two points.

💡 EXPLORING THE LESSON

In today's lesson, we will explore what the midpoint of a line segment is and how to find it. The midpoint is an important concept in Geometry because it allows us to locate the center point between two locations, whether on a graph, a map, or in real-world situations. We will learn how to find the midpoint by looking at a graph of a line segment or by using the coordinates of two endpoints. We will also learn how to find a missing endpoint when one endpoint and the midpoint are known. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to solve mathematical problems and real-world problems involving midpoints.


What is the Midpoint?

The midpoint of a line segment is the point that is exactly halfway between the two endpoints. It splits the segment into two equal parts. On a coordinate plane, you can find the midpoint visually by finding the middle between the x-values and the middle between the y-values.


How to Find the Midpoint on a Graph:

If you are given a graph, plot the two endpoints carefully. Then:

  • Find the halfway point horizontally (between the x-values).

  • Find the halfway point vertically (between the y-values).

  • The midpoint is where these two halfway points meet.


Example 1

Find the point of the line segment given on the graph shown.

Solution:

  • Find the halfway point horizontally: \(\frac{1}{2}(-7+5)=\frac{1}{2}(-2)=-1\)
  • Find the halfway point vertically: \(\frac{1}{2}(3+-4)=\frac{1}{2}(-1)=-0.5\)
  • Midpoint: \((-1,-0.5)\)



How to Find the Midpoint Using Coordinates:

If you know the coordinates of two endpoints \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\), use the Midpoint Formula.

\(Midpoint=\Large (\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\)


Example 1

Find the midpoint of the segment with endpoints \((2,4)\) and \((6,8)\).

Solution:

  • Identify the endpoints: \((x_1,y_1)\implies (2,4)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\implies (6,8)\).
  • Substitute the values of the endpoints into the midpoint formula.
  • \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})=(\frac{2+6}{2},\frac{4+8}{2})=(\frac{8}{2},\frac{12}{2})=(4,6)\)
  • Midpoint \(=(4,6)\)



Example 2 - Real-World

A straight path connects two benches in a park. One bench is at point \((1,3)\) and the other bench is at \((5,7)\) on the park’s map.

Where would you place a new trash can so it is exactly halfway between the two benches?

Solution:

  • Identify the endpoints: \((x_1,y_1)\implies (1,3)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\implies (5,7)\).
  • Substitute the values of the endpoints into the midpoint formula.
  • \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2},\frac{y_1+y_2}{2})=(\frac{1+5}{2},\frac{3+7}{2})=(\frac{6}{2},\frac{10}{2})=(3,5)\)
  • Midpoint \(=(3,5)\)

The trash can should be placed at \((3,5)\).



How to Find an Endpoint Given the Midpoint and One Endpoint:

Sometimes, you know one endpoint and the midpoint and need to find the missing endpoint.

Use the idea behind the Midpoint Formula:

  • Double the midpoint coordinates.

  • Subtract the known endpoint coordinates to find the missing endpoint.

Formulas

\(x_2=2M_x-x_1\)

\(y_2=2M_y-y_1\)


Example 3

The midpoint of a line segment is \((4,5)\) and one endpoint is \((2,3)\).

Find the missing endpoint.

Solution:

  • Identify the endpoint and the midpoint: \((x_1,y_1)\implies (2,3)\) and \((M_x,M_y)\implies (4,5)\).
  • Substitute the values of the endpoints into the formulas to find \(x_2\) and \(y_2\) of the other endpoint.
  • \(x_2=2\cdot 4-2 \implies x_2=8-2 \implies x_2=6\)
  • \(y_2=2\cdot 5-3 \implies y_2=10-3 \implies y_2=7\)
  • Endpoint \(=(6,7)\)



Example 4 - Real-World

A construction worker marks the center point between two fence posts at \((10,15.5)\). One post is already placed at \((6,9)\).

Where should the second post be placed?

Solution:

  • Identify the endpoint and the midpoint: \((x_1,y_1)\implies (6,9)\) and \((M_x,M_y)\implies (10,15.5)\).
  • Substitute the values of the endpoints into the formulas to find \(x_2\) and \(y_2\) of the other endpoint.
  • \(x_2=2\cdot 10-6 \implies x_2=20-6 \implies x_2=14\)
  • \(y_2=2\cdot 15.5-9 \implies y_2=31-9 \implies y_2=22\)
  • Endpoint \(=(14,22)\)

The second post should be placed at \((14,22)\).



Conclusion

Finding the midpoint of a line segment is a useful tool both in mathematics and in everyday life. Whether using a graph or the Midpoint Formula, you can locate the center between two points accurately. Understanding how to find a missing endpoint given a midpoint and one endpoint is also important for solving geometric and real-world problems. With practice, you will become confident in finding midpoints, interpreting them in real-world contexts, and using them to solve new types of problems.
