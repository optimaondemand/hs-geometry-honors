📚 VOCABULARY

Acute Triangle: A triangle where all three interior angles are less than 90 degrees.

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional surface formed by two perpendicular number lines called axes that intersect at the origin.

Coordinates: An ordered pair of numbers (x, y) that identifies the location of a point on the coordinate plane.

Distance Formula: A formula used to determine the distance between two points in a plane: \(d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}\).

Equilateral Triangle: A triangle with three sides of equal length and three angles of equal measure.

Hypotenuse: The side opposite the right angle in a right triangle; it is the longest side.

Isosceles Triangle: A triangle with at least two sides of equal length.

Obtuse Triangle: A triangle that has one angle greater than 90 degrees.

Origin: The point \((0,0)\) where the x-axis and y-axis intersect on the coordinate plane.

Right Triangle: A triangle that has one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees.

Scalene Triangle: A triangle with all sides of different lengths.

Side Length: The measurement of a side of a triangle or polygon.

Slope: The measure of the steepness of a line, calculated by the ratio of vertical change to horizontal change.

Slope Formula: A formula used to find the slope between two points: \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \).

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore how to find important features of a triangle when it is presented on a coordinate plane. Specifically, we will learn how to find the centroid, the medians, and the midsegment of a triangle. The centroid is the point where the three medians of a triangle intersect. A median is a line segment that connects a vertex of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side. A midsegment is a line segment that connects the midpoints of two sides of a triangle. In this lesson, you will either plot points on a graph or identify and label coordinates for centroids, medians, and midsegments by calculation.


Finding the Centroid of a Triangle

The centroid is the point where the three medians of a triangle meet. It is often called the “center of gravity” of the triangle.

To find the centroid when given the vertices of a triangle \((x_1,y_1)\), \((x_2,y_2)\), and \((x_3,y_3)\), use the Centroid Formula:

\(\Large (\frac{x_1+x_2+x_3}{3},\frac{y_1+y_2+y_3}{3})\)

Steps:

  1. Add all the x-coordinates together and divide by 3.

  2. Add all the y-coordinates together and divide by 3.

  3. Plot the resulting point as the centroid or identify its coordinates.



Example 1

Given \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) shown, find the coordinates of the centroid.

Solution:

  • Let's begin by identifying the coordinates of the vertices of \(\bigtriangleup ABC\).
    • \(A(2,3)\), \(B(5,7)\), and \(C(10,4)\)
  • Next, we will add all the x-coordinates together and divide by 3.
    • \(\frac{2+5+10}{3} \implies \frac{17}{3}\)
    • Note - If the numerator is not divisible evenly by 3, leave it as a fraction unless otherwise stated.
  • Now, we will add together all the y-coordinates and divide by 3.
    • \(\frac{3+7+4}{3} \implies \frac{14}{3}\)
  • The order pair representing the coordinates of the centroid for \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) is \((\frac{17}{3}, \frac{14}{3})\).



Finding the Midsegment of a Triangle

A midsegment is a segment connecting the midpoints of two sides of a triangle.

Steps to Find a Midsegment:

  1. Find the midpoints of two sides using the Midpoint Formula: \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

  2. Plot the midpoints on the coordinate plane.

  3. Draw the segment connecting these two midpoints.

  4. The midsegment is parallel to the third side and half its length.



Example 2

Plot the midsegment that is parallel to side \(AC\) of \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) shown.

Solution:

  • If we need to graph the midsegment that is parallel to \(\overline{AC} \), then we need to find the midpoints of \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{AC}\).
  • So, we will begin by finding the midpoints using the midpoint formula \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).
  • Let's identify the coordinates of the vertices for the triangle.
    • \(A(3,1)\), \(B(5,7)\), and \(C(11,5)\)
  • Now, we will find the midpoint of \(\overline{AB}\) using \(A(3,1)\) and \(B(5,7)\).
    • \(Midpoint=(\frac{3+5}{2},\frac{1+7}{2})=(\frac{8}{2},\frac{8}{2})=(4,4)\)
  • Next, we need to find the midpoint of \(\overline{AC}\) using \(A(3,1)\) and \(C(11,5)\).
    • \(Midpoint=(\frac{3+11}{2},\frac{1+5}{2})=(\frac{14}{2},\frac{6}{2})=(7,3)\)
  • Using the coordinates for the midpoints of \(\overline{AB}\) and \(\overline{AC}\), we will plot this on the coordinate plane.
  • Then we connect these with a segment that will represent the Midsegment.
  • Points D and E repesent the midpoints \((4,4)\) and \((7,3)\).
  • \(\overline{DE}\) is the Midsegment of \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) and is parallel to \(\overline{AC}\)



Example 3

Find the coordinates of points D and E that represent the Midsegment \(\overline{DE}\) of \(\bigtriangleup ABC\).

Solution:

  • Since points D and E are the endpoints of the Midsegment \(\overline{DE}\), then they are midpoints.
  • Point D is the midpoint of \(\overline{BC}\). To find its coordinates, we can use the midpoint formula \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).
    • \(B(5,7)\) and \(C(8,3)\)
    • \(D=(\frac{5+8}{2},\frac{7+3}{2})=(\frac{13}{2},\frac{10}{2})=(6.5,5)\)
  • Point E is the midpoint of \(\overline{AC}\). To find its coordinates, we can use the midpoint formula \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).
    • \(A(2,3)\) and \(C(8,3)\)
    • \(E=(\frac{2+8}{2},\frac{3+3}{2})=(\frac{10}{2},\frac{6}{2})=(5,3)\)
  • The coordinates of the endpoints of the midsegment \(\overline{DE}\) are \(D=(6.5,5)\) and \(E=(5,3)\).



Finding the Medians of a Triangle

A median connects a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

Steps to Find a Median:

  1. Find the midpoint of the side opposite the vertex using the Midpoint Formula \((\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2})\).

  2. Draw or describe the segment connecting the vertex to that midpoint.


Writing the Equation of a Median

Once you know the two points that form a median (the vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side), you can find the equation of the line that represents the median.

Steps to Write the Equation of a Median:

  1. Find the slope of the median using the Slope Formula \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\), where \((x_1,y_1)\) is the vertex of the triangle and \((x_2,y_2)\) is the midpoint of the side opposite that vertex.

  2. Use the point-slope form of a line, \(y-y_1=m(x-x_1)\), where \(m\) is the slope and \((x_1,y_1)\) is the vertex, to write the equation of the line containing the median.



Example 4

Find and draw the median from \(\angle B\) for \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) shown.

Solution:

  • To find the median from \(\angle B\), we need to first find the midpoint on the side opposite \(\angle B\).
  • We will begin by finding the midpoint of \(\overline{AC}\).
    • \(A(2,2)\) and \(C(11,6)\)
    • Midpoint\(=(\frac{2+11}{2},\frac{2+6}{2})=(\frac{13}{2},\frac{8}{2})=(6.5,4)\)
  • The median from \(\angle B\) will be a segment that connects point B with the midpoint on \(\overline{AC}\).
  • Now that we have found the midpoint, we will plot this point. Then draw a segment from point B to that point.



Example 5

Write the equation of the line containing the median from \(\angle A\) for \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) shown.

Solution:

  • We begin by finding the midpoint of the side opposite \(\angle A\).
  • To find the midpoint of \(\overline{BC}\), we will use the points \(B(7,8)\) and \(C(11,6)\).
    • Midpoint\(=(\frac{7+11}{2},\frac{8+6}{2})=(\frac{18}{2},\frac{14}{2})=(9,7)\)
  • Next, we will find the slope of the line using the midpoint \((9,7)\) and vertex A \((2,2)\).
    • \(m=\frac{7-2}{9-2}=\frac{5}{7}\)
  • Last, we will use the slope \(m=\frac{5}{7}\) and vertex A \((2,2)\) to write the equation using point-slope form.
    • \(y-2=\frac{5}{7}(x-2)\)

The equation of the line containing the median from \(\angle A\) is \(y-2=\frac{5}{7}(x-2)\).



Conclusion

Finding important points and segments of a triangle on the coordinate plane helps us understand the triangle’s structure. The centroid shows the balance point, the medians connect vertices to midpoints, and the midsegment connects two midpoints and reveals parallelism. Writing the equation of a median line allows us to describe the median mathematically. By plotting points, finding midpoints, calculating slopes, and using formulas, you can accurately find and represent centroids, medians, midsegments, and median equations. With practice, you will be able to confidently work with these important triangle features!
