📚 VOCABULARY

Algebraic Rule: A mathematical expression that shows how each point’s coordinates change during a transformation; written in the form \((x,y)\rightarrow (x',y')\).

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional grid formed by the intersection of a horizontal axis (x-axis) and a vertical axis (y-axis).

Dilation: A transformation that changes the size of a figure using a scale factor, while preserving shape and angle measures.

Image: The resulting figure after a transformation has been applied to the pre-image.

Nonrigid Motion: A transformation that changes the size of a figure but preserves shape and angle measures, such as a dilation.

Pre-Image: The original figure before a transformation occurs.

Reflection: A transformation where a figure is flipped over a line, producing a mirror image.

Rigid Motion: A transformation that preserves size and shape, such as translations, reflections, and rotations.

Rotation: A transformation where a figure is turned around a fixed point at a specific angle and direction.

Scale Factor: The number by which the coordinates of a figure are multiplied to perform a dilation.

Sequence of Transformations: A set of two or more transformations applied in a specific order to a figure.

Similarity: A relationship between two figures that have the same shape but not necessarily the same size; angle measures are equal and side lengths are proportional.

Transformation: A change in the position, size, or orientation of a figure in the coordinate plane.

Translation: A transformation that slides a figure a specific distance in a specific direction.

Vector: A quantity that shows both direction and magnitude; used to describe translations on the coordinate plane.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to graph figures that undergo sequences of transformations in the coordinate plane. Transformations include translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations. Each transformation affects the figure in different ways: some change its location, others its orientation or size. You'll review how each type of transformation looks on the coordinate plane, what happens to the figure's coordinates, and what algebraic rule is used to describe the transformation. Then you'll learn how to graph the result of a figure that undergoes more than one transformation in a specific order.


Transformation Review

Translation Reflection Rotation Dilation
Slides a figure horizontally and/or vertically. Flips a figure over a line (like the x-axis, y-axis, or line \(y=x\)). Turns a figure around a point, typically the origin. Enlarges or reduces the size of a figure using a scale factor \(k\).

Algebraic Rule:

\((x,y)\rightarrow (x+a,y+b)\)

Algebraic Rules:

Over x-axis: \((x,y)\rightarrow (x,-y)\)

Over y-axis: \((x,y)\rightarrow (-x,y)\)

Over line \(y=x\): \((x,y)\rightarrow (y,x)\)

Algebraic Rules:

90 degrees counterclockwise (270 clockwise): \((x,y)\rightarrow (-y,x)\)

180 degrees: \((x,y)\rightarrow (-x,-y)\)

270 degrees counterclockwise (90 clockwise): \((x,y)\rightarrow (y,-x)\)

Algebraic Rule:

\((x,y)\rightarrow (kx,ky)\)



✏️ Graphing a Sequence of Transformations

To graph a sequence of transformations:

  1. Apply the first transformation to each vertex of the pre-image.

  2. Use the resulting coordinates as the input for the second transformation.

  3. Plot all image points after each transformation step.

  4. Connect the points to form the transformed figure.



Example 1

Triangle ABC on the graph shown is translated 4 units right and 1 unit down, then reflected over the y-axis.

Draw the transformed image triangle A'B'C'.

Solution:

Transformations:

  1. Translate the triangle 4 units right and 1 unit down.

  2. Reflect the resulting figure over the y-axis.

\(A(-6,1)\), \(B(-3,6)\), and \(C(-2,-2)\)

Step 1 – Translation
Apply \((x,y)\rightarrow (x+4, y-1)\)

  • \(A(-6,1)\rightarrow A'(-6+4,1-1)=(-2,0)\)
  • \(B(-3,6)\rightarrow B'(-3+4,6-1)=(1,5)\)
  • \(C(-2,-2)\rightarrow C'(-2+4,-2-1)=(2,-3)\)

Step 2 – Reflection over y-axis
Apply \((x,y)\rightarrow (-x,y)\)

  • \(A'(-2,0)\rightarrow A''(2,0)\)
  • \(B'(1,5)\rightarrow B''(-1,5)\)
  • \(C'(2,-3)\rightarrow C''(-2,-3)\)

Graph: Plot the coordinates from step 2, then use the polygon tool to connect the points and draw the final image.



Example 2

Quadrilateral ABCD on the graph shown is dilated by a scale factor of 2, then rotated \(90^\circ\) counterclockwise about the origin.

Draw the transformed image A'B'C'D'.

Solution:

Transformations:

  1. Dilate the figure from the origin with a scale factor of 2.
  2. Rotate the resulting figure 90° counterclockwise about the origin.

\(A(-5,2)\), \(B(-1,6)\), \(C(2,3)\), and \(D(-2,-1)\)

Step 1 – Dilation
Apply \((x,y)\rightarrow (2x,2y)\)

  • \(A(-5,2)\rightarrow A'(-10,4)\)
  • \(B(-1,6)\rightarrow B'(-2,12)\)
  • \(C(2,3)\rightarrow C'(4,6)\)
  • \(D(-2,-1)\rightarrow D'(-4,-2)\)

Step 2 – Rotation \(90^\circ\) Counterclockwise
Apply \((x,y)\rightarrow (-y,x)\)

  • \(A'(-10,4)\rightarrow A''(-4,-10)\)
  • \(B'(-2,12)\rightarrow B''(-12,-2)\)
  • \(C'(4,6)\rightarrow C''(-6,4)\)
  • \(D'(-4,-2)\rightarrow D''(2,-4)\)

Graph: Plot the coordinates from step 2, then use the polygon tool to connect the points and draw the final image.



Conclusion

In this lesson, you reviewed the four main transformations—translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations—and learned their verbal meanings, coordinate changes, and algebraic rules. You then practiced applying two transformations in a row to a single figure, plotting each stage of the sequence. By mastering how to graph a sequence of transformations step-by-step, you can now visualize how a shape changes in size, position, and orientation—and explain the reasoning behind each transformation in a sequence.
