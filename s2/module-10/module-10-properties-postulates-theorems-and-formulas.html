Properties

Angle Preservation Property:

  • All transformations preserve angle measures.
  • Dilations preserve the measures of angles and the ratios of side lengths.

Dilation Property: Dilations with a scale factor ≠ 1 produce similar figures by enlarging or reducing the figure proportionally.

Dilation Property: Dilation is a non-rigid motion, meaning it changes size but preserves shape. Algebraic Rule: \( (x,y) \longrightarrow (kx, ky) \), where \(k\) is the scale factor.

Dilation and Similarity

  • Dilations are non-rigid motions that preserve shape but not size.
  • Dilations with scale factor  enlarge, and  reduce.
  • A dilation followed by a rigid motion results in a similar figure.

Key Theorems and Properties

  • Congruence Mapping Theorem: A figure is congruent to another if there exists a sequence of rigid motions that maps one onto the other.
  • Similarity Transformation Theorem: A figure is similar to another if there exists a sequence of rigid motions and a dilation that maps one onto the other.
  • Angle Preservation: All transformations preserve angle measure.
  • Parallelism and Collinearity: Preserved in all transformations.

Reversibility: Every transformation has an inverse transformation that undoes it.

Rigid Motion Properties

  • Translations, rotations, and reflections are rigid motions.
  • Rigid motions preserve distance, angle measure, and orientation.
  • A sequence of rigid motions maps a figure onto a congruent figure.

Rotation Rules (about the origin):

  • 90° counterclockwise (or 270° clockwise): \((x,y) \longrightarrow (-y,x) \)
  • 180°: \( (x,y) \longrightarrow (-x,-y) \)
  • 270° counterclockwise (or 90° clockwise): \( (x,y) \longrightarrow (y,-x) \)

Rigid Motion Property: Reflections and rotations are rigid motions because they preserve distance and angle measure.

Transformation Properties

  • Translations, reflections, and rotations are rigid motions, preserving shape, size, and angle measures.
  • Dilations are non-rigid motions, preserving angle measures and proportionality but not side lengths.

Translation Property: Translation is a rigid motion, meaning it preserves distance, angle measure, and orientation. Algebraic Rule: \( (x,y) \longrightarrow (x+a, y+b) \)



Postulates

Rigid Motion Postulate: Reflections, rotations, and translations preserve distance and angle measure (congruence).

Congruence Postulate for Rigid Motion: If a figure is translated, the pre-image and image are congruent.



Theorems

Angle Preservation Property: All transformations preserve the angle measures of a figure.

Composition of Two Reflections Across Intersecting Lines — If two lines intersect at a point (P) at angle (\(\theta \)), then the composition of reflections across the two lines is a rotation of (\(2\theta \)) about (P). As a corollary: any figure with two or more lines of reflection symmetry through a common point also has rotational symmetry.

Congruence Mapping Theorem: A sequence of rigid motions (including reflections and rotations) maps any figure onto a congruent figure.

Congruence Transformation Theorem: A composition of rigid motions maps a figure to a congruent figure.

Distance Preservation Property: Only rigid motions preserve the length of segments.

Orientation Property: Reflections change the orientation (clockwise/counterclockwise), while translations and rotations preserve it.

Reflection Symmetry of Regular Polygons — A regular polygon with (n) sides has exactly (n) lines of reflection symmetry.

Rigid Motion Congruence Theorem: A sequence of rigid motions maps a figure to a congruent figure.

Rotational Symmetry of Regular Polygons — A regular polygon with (n) sides has rotational symmetry of order (n), with angle of rotation \(\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}\).

Similarity Transformation Theorem: A sequence including a dilation (with or without rigid motions) maps a figure to a similar figure.

Similarity Transformation Theorem: A composition of rigid motions and dilations maps a figure to a similar figure.

Similarity Transformation Theorem: A dilation maps a figure to a similar figure.

  • If \(k>1\), the image is an enlargement.
  • If (0<k<1\), the image is a reduction.

Translation Symmetry of Tessellations — Every tessellation of the plane has at least one translation symmetry; equivalently, any pattern with translation symmetry tiles the plane.
