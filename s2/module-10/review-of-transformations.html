📚 VOCABULARY

Congruence: Congruence means that two shapes are exactly the same in size and shape. This means you could move one shape over the other, and they would fit perfectly on top of each other without stretching or compressing.

Dilation: Dilation is a transformation that changes the size of a shape but keeps its proportions the same. It either makes the shape larger or smaller using a specific point called the center of dilation and a multiplier known as the scale factor.

Image: The image is the new shape that you get after you have transformed the original shape (called the preimage) by moving, resizing, or rotating it. Think of it as the result or outcome of the transformation.

Preimage: The preimage is the original shape before any transformations are applied to it. It's the starting point from which we create the image through transformations like translation, reflection, or dilation.

Reflection: A reflection is like placing a mirror along a line and flipping a shape over that line. The shape looks the same but is reversed, like seeing your mirror image.

Rigid Transformation: A rigid transformation changes the position of a shape without altering its size or shape. Types of rigid transformations include translations, rotations, and reflections. These transformations maintain the congruence between the preimage and the image.

Rotation: A rotation turns a shape around a fixed point, called the center of rotation, through a specific angle and direction (like clockwise or counterclockwise). The shape stays the same size and shape; it just spins around.

Scale Factor: The scale factor is a number that you multiply by the dimensions of the original shape (preimage) to get the dimensions of the new shape (image) during a dilation. A scale factor greater than 1 makes the shape larger, and a scale factor less than 1 makes it smaller.

Similarity: Two shapes are similar if they have the same shape but different sizes. This means they can be resized versions of each other, maintaining the proportions of their sides and angles. Similarity is achieved through transformations like dilation.

Translation: A translation moves every point of a shape the same distance in the same direction. The shape looks exactly the same, just in a different place. It's like sliding a shape across the floor or shifting it up, down, left, or right.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today's lesson, we will explore all fundamental types of geometric transformations: translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations. These transformations allow us to manipulate polygons on a flat plane, altering their positions and orientations while preserving their shapes. By understanding how to identify these transformations, you'll gain insights into the symmetry and design seen in art and nature, and develop the ability to solve geometric problems more effectively.


Translations

A translation moves a polygon in a straight line from one place to another on a flat plane, keeping its orientation, size, and shape unchanged. The direction of the translation can be left, right, up, or down. Moves every point of a shape the same distance in the same direction. The shape does not turn or rotate at all; it simply shifts position on the plane. Imagine sliding a book across a table from one point to another without turning it. This is a translation.

  • Shape and Size: The shape and size of the figure remain unchanged during a translation. If you start with a large rectangle, you end with a large rectangle.
  • Angles: The angles within the shape remain the same. For example, a right angle in a rectangle remains a right angle after translation.
  • Parallelism: If two lines on the original shape were parallel, they remain parallel after the translation.


Left or Right Translations:

    • If you slide a quadrilateral to the right, every point moves to the right.
    • If you slide a quadrilateral to the left, every point moves to the left.

Up or Down Translations:

    • If you slide a triangle up, every point moves up.
    • If you slide a triangle down, every point moves down.


Examples:

Quadrilateral ABCD was translated to the right, creating quadrilateral A'B'C'D'.

Triangle GHI was translated down to create triangle G'H'I'.



Reflections

A reflection flips a polygon over a line on the flat plane. The line can be vertical, horizontal, or diagonal. This changes the orientation of the shape. For example, if the left side of a shape was facing you before, it might be facing away from you after a reflection over a vertical line. Points on the shape move perpendicularly towards or away from the line of reflection, flipping to the opposite side of the line.

  • Shape and Size: Like translations, reflections do not alter the size or the fundamental geometry of the shape. A small circle remains a small circle after reflection.
  • Angles: Internal angles remain the same before and after reflection. For example, a 45-degree angle in a polygon remains at 45 degrees after it is reflected.
  • Proportions: The proportions within the shape are preserved. If one side of a triangle is twice as long as another before reflection, the same relationship holds after reflection.

Vertical Reflections:

    • Example: Reflecting a quadrilateral over a vertical line means the left side becomes the right, and the right side becomes the left.

Quadrilateral ABCD was reflected over a vertical line to create quadrilateral A'B'C'D'.

Horizontal Reflections:

    • Example: A horizontal reflection of a triangle flips it over a horizontal line, switching the top to the bottom.

Triangle GHI was reflected over a horizontal line, creating triangle G'H'I'.

Diagonal Reflections:

    • Example: When you reflect a triangle over a diagonal line, the reflection swaps its position diagonally, mirroring the shape across the line.

Triangle GHI was reflected over a diagonal line to create triangle G'H'I'.



What is a Rotation?

A rotation is a transformation that turns a polygon around a fixed point, often called the center of rotation. The shape rotates by a certain angle and in a specific direction, either clockwise or counterclockwise. Unlike moving straight or flipping, rotation involves spinning the shape around.

Imagine turning a key in a lock. The key rotates around the center of the lock, just like a polygon rotates around its center point.


Characteristics That Stay the Same in a Rotation

When a polygon is rotated, several features remain unchanged:

  • Shape and Size: The polygon maintains its original shape and size during a rotation. If you start with a small square, you end with a small square, just possibly facing a different direction.
  • Angles: The internal angles of the polygon do not change. For example, the right angles in a rectangle remain right angles after rotation.
  • Distances Between Points: The distances between any points on the polygon remain the same. If two points on a triangle are 5 cm apart before rotation, they are still 5 cm apart afterward.
Quadrilateral ABCD was rotated \(180^\circ\) about a point to create quadrilateral A'B'C'D'. Polygon FGHIJ was rotated \(90^\circ\) about a point to create polygon F'G'H'I'J'.



Understanding Dilations

Dilation is a transformation that changes the size of a figure but preserves its shape. The scale factor determines how much the figure is enlarged or reduced:

  • Dilation Greater than 1: When the scale factor is greater than 1, the image is larger than the preimage. This is called an enlargement. For example, if the scale factor is 2, every length in the preimage is doubled in the image.

  • Dilation Less than 1: When the scale factor is less than 1, the image is smaller than the preimage. This is known as a reduction. For example, a scale factor of 0.5 means every length in the preimage is halved in the image.
The preimage FGHI was dilated by a factor of \(\dfrac{1}{2}\) to create the image F'G'H'I'.

The preimage ABCD has been dilated by a factor of 2 to create the image A'B'C'D'.



Conclusion

Today's exploration of translations, reflections, rotations, and dilations should equip you with the ability to distinguish between these transformations and understand how they affect polygons. Remember, while these transformations preserve the shape and size of figures, translations maintain the orientation and position of the shape in a consistent direction, whereas reflections invert the orientation relative to a line. Recognizing these differences and unchanged properties will help you master the concepts of geometry in practical and theoretical applications. Remember, translations slide a polygon without rotating or flipping it, and reflections flip a polygon over a specific line. These transformations are crucial for creating symmetrical designs and solving geometric problems. Try using different polygons and applying these transformations to see their effects on the shapes around you. This will not only help in your studies but also give you a deeper appreciation for the geometry in everyday life.
