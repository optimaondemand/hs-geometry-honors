📚 VOCABULARY

Algebraic Rule: A mathematical expression that shows how each point’s coordinates change during a transformation; written in the form \((x,y)\rightarrow (x',y')\).

Coordinate Plane: A two-dimensional grid formed by the intersection of a horizontal axis (x-axis) and a vertical axis (y-axis).

Dilation: A transformation that changes the size of a figure using a scale factor, while preserving shape and angle measures.

Image: The resulting figure after a transformation has been applied to the pre-image.

Pre-Image: The original figure before a transformation occurs.

Reflection: A transformation where a figure is flipped over a line, producing a mirror image.

Rigid Motion: A transformation that preserves size and shape, such as translations, reflections, and rotations.

Rotation: A transformation where a figure is turned around a fixed point at a specific angle and direction.

Scale Factor: The number by which the coordinates of a figure are multiplied to perform a dilation.

Sequence of Transformations: A set of two or more transformations applied in a specific order to a figure.

Similarity: A relationship between two figures that have the same shape but not necessarily the same size; angle measures are equal and side lengths are proportional.

Transformation: A change in the position, size, or orientation of a figure in the coordinate plane.

Translation: A transformation that slides a figure a specific distance in a specific direction.

Vector: A quantity that shows both direction and magnitude; used to describe translations on the coordinate plane.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to describe and identify sequences of transformations in the coordinate plane. You will learn how figures move, change size, or flip by combining transformations such as reflections, rotations, translations, and dilations. We’ll review each transformation with a verbal description of what it does to a figure and how to recognize it on the coordinate grid. Then, we’ll study two examples where a triangle is transformed step-by-step, and you’ll identify which transformations occurred and in what order.


Transformation Review

Translation Reflection Rotation Dilation
Slides a figure horizontally and/or vertically. Flips a figure over a line (like the x-axis, y-axis, or line \(y=x\)). Turns a figure around a point, typically the origin. Enlarges or reduces the size of a figure using a scale factor \(k\).

Algebraic Rule:

\((x,y)\rightarrow (x+a,y+b)\)

Algebraic Rules:

Over x-axis: \((x,y)\rightarrow (x,-y)\)

Over y-axis: \((x,y)\rightarrow (-x,y)\)

Over line \(y=x\): \((x,y)\rightarrow (y,x)\)

Algebraic Rules:

90 degrees counterclockwise (270 clockwise): \((x,y)\rightarrow (-y,x)\)

180 degrees: \((x,y)\rightarrow (-x,-y)\)

270 degrees counterclockwise (90 clockwise): \((x,y)\rightarrow (y,-x)\)

Algebraic Rule:

\((x,y)\rightarrow (kx,ky)\)



Example 1

The graph shows triangle ABC and its transformed image, triangle A'B'C'.

Which sequence of transformations maps \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) onto \(\bigtriangleup A'B'C'\)?

A. The figure was rotated 90° counterclockwise, then translated 3 units left.
B. The figure was reflected over the y-axis, and dilated by a scale factor of 2.
C. The figure was translated 5 units right, then dilated by a factor of \(\frac{3}{2}\).​
D. The figure was dilated by a factor of \(\frac{3}{2}\), and reflected over the line \(y=x\).

Solution:

First, observe the size of the triangle: the image is larger than the pre-image, which suggests a dilation occurred. Comparing coordinates by dividing x' by x and y' by y, each point of the image is \(\frac{3}{2}\) times further from the origin, confirming a scale factor of \(\frac{3}{2}\).

Then, observe the orientation of the triangle. The triangle is not just enlarged—it’s been flipped and reoriented. We can test the different algebraic rules for the 3 types of reflections to help identify the reflection. The reflection is over the line \(y=x\), which swaps the x- and y-coordinates.



Example 2

The graph shows triangle ABC and its transformed image, triangle A'B'C'.

Which sequence of transformations maps \(\bigtriangleup ABC\) onto \(\bigtriangleup A'B'C'\)?

A. The figure was reflected over the x-axis, and translated left 5 units.
B. The figure was rotated \(180^\circ\), then translated 2 units down.
C. The figure was reflected over the y-axis, then dilated by a factor of \(\frac{1}{2}\).​
D. The figure was rotated \(90^\circ\) clockwise, then reflected over the x-axis.

Solution:

Start by noting that the size of the triangles stays the same—so no dilation occurred, meaning this is a sequence of rigid motions.
Next, compare corresponding points: the triangle has been flipped over the x-axis (y-values change sign), then shifted left 5 units (x-values all decrease by 5).
So, the correct sequence is a reflection over the x-axis, followed by a translation 5 units left.



Conclusion

In this lesson, you learned how to identify single and multiple transformations by analyzing the coordinates and orientation of figures on the coordinate plane. By comparing pre-image and image points, you can determine whether a figure has been translated, reflected, rotated, or dilated—and in what order. Whether you're seeing a change in size, position, or orientation, the key is to apply transformation rules and look for consistent patterns in coordinates. Understanding sequences of transformations is essential for recognizing congruence, similarity, and how shapes relate through motion.
