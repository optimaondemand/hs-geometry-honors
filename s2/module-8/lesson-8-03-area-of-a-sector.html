📚 VOCABULARY

Arc: A curved portion of a circle’s circumference between two points on the circle.

Area: The measure of the space enclosed within a two-dimensional shape, expressed in square units.

Area of a Sector: The portion of a circle’s total area enclosed by two radii and an arc, found using a fraction of the circle’s total area; \(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\).

Center: The point in a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is at the center of a circle and whose sides extend to the circumference.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle but does not necessarily pass through the center.

Circle: A set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Degree: A unit of measurement for angles, where a full circle measures \(360^\circ\).

Intercepted Arc: The arc that lies between the two sides of an angle, often measured by the angle's degree.

Major Arc: An arc that measures more than \(180^\circ\).

Minor Arc: An arc that measures less than \(180^\circ\).

Pi (π): A mathematical constant approximately equal to 3.14159, representing the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter.

Proportion: A mathematical statement that two ratios are equal, often used to solve for missing values.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle’s circumference.

Sector: A region of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore what the area of a sector is and how to calculate it. A sector is a portion of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc, similar to a "slice" of a pizza or pie. The area of a sector depends on both the radius of the circle and the central angle that defines the sector. This concept is useful in various real-world applications, such as determining the area of circular gardens, measuring land plots, and designing curved roads. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to calculate the area of a sector, determine the radius or central angle when the sector’s area is known, and apply these skills to solve real-world problems.


Finding the Area of a Sector

The area of a sector is a fraction of the total area of a circle.

Where the central angle is given in degrees, the formula used is:

\(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\)

where:

  • \(A\) is the area of the sector,
  • \(\theta\) is the central angle in degrees,
  • \(r\) is the radius of the circle.



Example 1

In \(\odot A\), the length of the radius is 4 units and \(m\angle CAB=154^\circ\).

Find the area of the sector.

Solution:

  • To find the area of the sector created by \(\angle CAB=154^\circ\), we can use the formula \(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\).
  • The length of the raidus is \(r=4\) and the measure of the angle is \(\theta =154^\circ\).
  • We substitute these values into the formula.
  • \(A=\frac{154}{360}\cdot \pi (4)^2 \implies A=\frac{154}{360}(16\pi) \implies A=\frac{77}{180}(16\pi) \implies A=\frac{77\cdot 16\pi}{180} \implies A=\frac{308\pi}{45}\)
  • The exact area of the sector is \(A=\frac{308\pi}{45}\;units^2\).
  • We can plug that into a calculator and get an approximate answer depending on what the question asks for.
    • Be sure to use the pi key on the calculator and not an estimation of pi. This gives a better approximation.
  • The approximate area of the sector is \(A\approx 21.502\;units^2\).



Example 2

In \(\odot A\), the area of the sector \(A=\frac{117\pi}{10}\;units^2\) and \(m\angle EDF=117^\circ\).

Find the length of the radius.

Solution:

  • To find the length of the radius for the sector created by \(\angle CAB=154^\circ\), we can use the formula \(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\).
  • We will substitute in the values we know and solve for the radius, \(r\).
  • \(\frac{117\pi}{10}=\frac{117}{360}\cdot \pi r^2 \implies \frac{117\pi}{10}=\frac{117\pi}{360}\cdot r^2\)
  • Now, we can use the reciprocal of \(\frac{117\pi}{360}\) to multiply to the other side to isolate \(r^2\).
  • \(\frac{360}{117\pi}\cdot \frac{117\pi}{10}=r^2\)
  • The \(117\pi\) in the numerator and denominator will cancel out.
  • \(\frac{360}{1}\cdot \frac{1}{10}=r^2 \implies \frac{360}{10}=r^2 \implies 36=r^2 \implies r=6\)
  • We find that the length of the radius is 6 units.



Example 3

A large pizza has a radius of 14 inches.

If a single slice represents a \(30^\circ\) sector, what is the area of one slice? Round your answer to the nearest hundredth.

Solution:

  • To find the area of the sector created by 1 slice of pizza, we will use the formula \(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\).
  • The radius of the pizza is \(r=14\). A single slice makes an angle where \(\theta =30^\circ\).
  • Now, we substitute those values into the equation.
  • \(A=\frac{30}{360}\cdot \pi (14)^2 \implies A=\frac{3}{36}(196\pi) \implies A=\frac{1\cdot 196\pi}{12} \implies A=\frac{49\pi}{3}\)
  • To find the approximate area, you want to find the exact area first. Then, plug that into your calculator at once to get a single estimation.
    • DO NOT plug things in at step one and use estimated values throughout the calculations. Your answer WILL be off in the end as this is truncated rounding.
    • Be sure to use the pi key on the calculator and not an estimation of pi. This gives a better approximation.
  • The approximate area of a slice of pizza is 51.31 square inches or \(51.31\;in^2\)



Example 4

A circular pool has a radius of 12 feet, and a shaded sector has an area of 107 square feet.

What is the central angle of the shaded sector? Round your answer to the nearest tenth of a degree.

Solution:

  • To find the measure of the central angle created by the sector, we will use the formula \(A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot \pi r^2\).
  • The raidus of the pool is \(r=12\). The shaded sector has an area of \(A=107\;ft^2\).
  • Now, we substitute those values into the equation.
  • \(107=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot \pi (12)^2 \implies 107=\frac{\theta}{360}(144\pi) \implies 107\div 144\pi =\frac{\theta}{360} \implies 360\cdot (107\div 144\pi)=\theta\)
  • To find the approximate measure of the angle, plug that into your calculator at once to get a single estimation.
    • DO NOT plug things in at step one and use estimated values throughout the calculations. Your answer WILL be off in the end as this is truncated rounding.
    • Be sure to use the pi key on the calculator and not an estimation of pi. This gives a better approximation.
  • The measure of the central angle of the shaded section of the pool is \(\approx 85.1^\circ\).



Conclusion

The area of a sector is a crucial concept in geometry with many practical applications. By understanding how to calculate sector area, as well as how to determine missing values like radius or central angle, we can solve problems in various fields, from landscaping and construction to food preparation. Whether measuring a pizza slice or designing a garden, using the correct formulas ensures accuracy and efficiency in calculations.
