📚 VOCABULARY

Altitude: A perpendicular segment from a vertex of a shape to the opposite side creating a right angle with the side.

Area: The measure of the space enclosed within a two-dimensional shape, expressed in square units.

Base: The bottom side of a geometric figure used as a reference for measurement.

Center: The point in a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Circle: A set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Diameter: A straight line passing through the center of a circle and touching both sides of the circumference, equal to twice the radius.

Height: The perpendicular distance from the base to the highest point of a shape.

Quadrilateral: A four-sided polygon with four angles.

Parallelogram: A quadrilateral with opposite sides that are parallel and equal in length.

Pi (π): A mathematical constant approximately equal to 3.14159, representing the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle’s circumference.

Rectangle: A quadrilateral with four right angles and opposite sides that are equal in length.

Rhombus: A parallelogram with all four sides of equal length.

Square: A quadrilateral with four equal sides and four right angles.

Trapezoid: A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides.

Triangle: A polygon with three sides and three angles.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore how to find the area of different two-dimensional geometric figures, including right triangles, triangles with an altitude, equilateral triangles, parallelograms, rectangles, squares, rhombuses, trapezoids, and circles. Understanding area is essential in both mathematical calculations and real-world applications, such as architecture, engineering, and design. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to apply the correct formula to find the area of these figures, solve practical problems, and determine missing measurements when given the area of a shape.


Finding the Area of a Triangle

The area of any triangle, if given the base and the altitude, can be found using the formula:

\(A=\frac{1}{2}bh\)

where \(b\) is the base and \(h\) is the height or altitude

  • For a right triangle, the two legs serve as the base and height, making it straightforward to apply the formula.
  • For a triangle with an altitude, the altitude must be identified or calculated before substituting it into the formula.


The area of an equilateral triangle, where all three sides are equal, can be found using the formula:

\(A=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2\)

where \(s\) is the length of one side


Example 1

Find the area of \(\bigtriangleup ABC\)

Solution:

  • To find the area of the triangle, we will use the formula \(A=\frac{1}{2}bh\).
  • We are given the base is 4 and the height is 6.
  • \(A=\frac{1}{2}(4)(6) \implies A=\frac{1}{2}(24) \implies A=12\;units^2\)
  • We find that the area is 12 square units.


Example 2

Find the area of \(\bigtriangleup DEF\).

Solution:

  • Since in the image it shows that all sides are equal, this is an equilateral triangle.
  • Therefore, we will use the formula \(A=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}s^2\).
  • In this triangle \(s=7\).
  • \(A=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(7)^2 \implies A=\frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4} \implies A\approx 21.22\; units^2\)
  • Make sure to pay attention to if an exact answer or rounded answer is desired.
  • The exact area is \(A=\frac{49\sqrt{3}}{4}\). The approximate answer is \(A=21.22\).


Example 3

Find the length of the base for \(\bigtriangleup GHI\) with a height of 7 and \(A=28\;units^2\).

Solution:

  • To find the length of the base, we will substitute what we know into the area formula and solve for what we do not know.
  • \(28=\frac{1}{2}(7)(b) \implies 28\cdot 2=2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(7)(b) \implies 56=7b \implies \frac{56}{7}=\frac{7b}{7} \implies b=8\)
  • By substituting in the area and the height, we can find that the base is 8.



Finding the Area of Quadrilaterals

  1. Parallelogram – The area of a parallelogram is found using the same formula as a triangle but without the division by 2:
    • \(A=bh\)
      • where the height is perpendicular to the base
  2. Rectangle – A rectangle is a special type of parallelogram where the height is one of its sides, so the formula simplifies to:
    • \(A=lw\)
      • where \(l\) is the length and \(w\) is the width
  3. Square – Since a square has all four sides equal, its area formula is:
    • \(A=s^2\)
      • where \(s\) is the side length
  4. Rhombus – The area of a rhombus can be found using its diagonals:
    • \(A=\frac{1}{2}d_1d_2\)
      • where \(d_1\) and \(d_2\) are the lengths of the diagonals
  5. Trapezoid – A trapezoid has only one pair of parallel sides. The formula for its area is:
    • \(A=\frac{1}{2}(b_1+b_2)h\)
      • where \(b_1\) and \(b_2\) are the lengths of the two parallel bases, and \(h\) is the height


Example 4

Find the area of parallelogram KLNM.

Solution:

  • To find the area of the parallelogram, we will use the formula \(A=bh\).
  • The base is 6 and the height is 3.7.
  • \(A=(6)(3.7) \implies A=22.2\).
  • We find that the area of the parallelogram is 22.2 square units.


Example 5

Find the value of the width in rectangle PQRS with \(A=28\;units^2\).

Solution:

  • To find the value of the width, we will use the formula \(A=lw\).
  • We will substitute in the value of the area, 28, and the value of the length, 7, then solve for the unknown width.
  • \(28=7w \implies \frac{28}{7}=\frac{7w}{7} \implies w=4\)
  • We find the width is 4 units.


Example 6

Find the area of square TUVW.

Solution:

  • To find the area of the square, we will use the formula \(A=s^2\).
  • The side length, s, is 5 units.
  • \(A=(5)^2 \implies A=25\)
  • We find that the area of the square is 25 square units.


Example 7

Find the length of diagonal \(\overline{CA}\) given \(A=47.45\;units^2\) and \(ZB=14.6\;  units\).

Solution:

  • To find the length of diagonal \(\overline{CA}\), we will substitute the values we know into the formula \(A=\frac{1}{2}d_1d_2\).
  • \(47.45=\frac{1}{2}(14.6)(d) \implies 2\cdot 47.45=2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(14.6)(d) \implies 94.9=14.6d \implies \frac{94.9}{14.6}=\frac{14.6d}{14.6} \implies d=6.5\)
  • We find that \(CA=6.5\).


Example 8

Find the length of the height of trapezoid DEFG given \(A=40\;units^2\), \(GF=6\;units\), and \(DE=14\;units\).

Solution:

  • To find the length of the height, we will substitute the values we know into the formula \(A=\frac{1}{2}(b_1+b_2)h\).
  • \(40=\frac{1}{2}(6+14)h \implies 2\cdot 40=2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(20)h \implies 80=20h \implies \frac{80}{20}=\frac{20h}{20} \implies h=4\)
  • We find that the height of the trapezoid is 4 units.



Finding the Area of a Circle

A circle’s area is calculated using the formula:

\(A=\pi r^2\)

where \(r\) is the radius of the circle


Example 9

Find the area of \(\odot H\).

Solution:

  • To find the area of the circle, we will use the formula \(A=\pi r^2\).
  • The radius is 4.4 units.
  • \(A=\pi (4.4)^2 \implies A=19.36\pi \implies A\approx 60.821\)
  • Be sure to pay attention if the question asks for the exact answer or the approximate answer.
  • The exact area is \(19.36\pi\).
  • The approximate area is \(60.821\).



Real-world Examples

Example 10

A city is constructing a new park with a trapezoidal flower bed. The shorter base of the flower bed is 8 meters (m), the longer base is 14 m, and the height of the flower bed is 6 m.

What is the total area of the flower bed that needs to be covered with soil?

Solution:

  • To find the total area of the flower bed, we will use the trapezoid area formula \(A=\frac{1}{2}(b_1+b_2)h\).
  • The lengths of the bases are 8 m and 14 m.
  • The height is 6 m.
  • \(A=\frac{1}{2}(8+14)(6) \implies A=\frac{1}{2}(22)(6) \implies A=\frac{1}{2}(132) \implies A=66 m^2\).
  • The total area to be covered in soil is \(66m^2\).


Example 11

A construction company is designing a triangular section of a bridge. The total area of the triangular section is 90 square feet, and the base of the triangle is 15 feet.

What is the height of the triangular section?

Solution:

  • To find the height of the triangular section, we will use the triangle area formula \(A=\frac{1}{2}bh\).
  • We know the area is 90 and the base is 15. We will substitute the values in and solve for the height, h.
  • \(90=\frac{1}{2}(15)(h) \implies 2\cdot 90=2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(15)(h) \implies 180=15h \implies \frac{180}{15}=\frac{15h}{15} \implies h=12m\)
  • We find that the height of the triangular section is 12m.


Conclusion

Understanding how to calculate the area of different geometric shapes is a fundamental skill in geometry and everyday life. Whether designing a building, creating artwork, or planning a sports field, area calculations help in making precise measurements and informed decisions. By identifying and applying the correct formulas, solving real-world problems, and using properties of shapes to find missing measurements, you will develop a deeper understanding of how geometry applies to practical situations.
