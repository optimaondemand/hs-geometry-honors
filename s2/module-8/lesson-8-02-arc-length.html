📚 VOCABULARY

Arc: A curved portion of a circle’s circumference between two points on the circle.

Arc Length: The measure of the distance along an arc, found using a portion of the circle’s circumference.

Center: The point in a circle that is equidistant from all points on the circle.

Central Angle: An angle whose vertex is at the center of a circle and whose sides extend to the circumference.

Chord: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle but does not necessarily pass through the center.

Circle: A set of all points in a plane that are equidistant from a given point called the center.

Circumference: The total distance around a circle, found using the formula \(C=2\pi r\).

Degree: A unit of measurement for angles, where a full circle measures \(360^\circ\).

Intercepted Arc: The arc that lies between the two sides of an angle, often measured by the angle's degree.

Major Arc: An arc that measures more than \(180^\circ\).

Minor Arc: An arc that measures less than \(180^\circ\).

Pi (π): A mathematical constant approximately equal to 3.14159, representing the ratio of a circle’s circumference to its diameter.

Proportion: A mathematical statement that two ratios are equal, often used to solve for missing values.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle’s circumference.

Sector: A region of a circle enclosed by two radii and an arc.

Segment of a Circle: The region of a circle bounded by a chord and the arc between the chord’s endpoints.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore what arc length is and how to calculate it. Arc length is the measure of a portion of a circle’s circumference. This concept is useful in many real-world applications, such as designing curved roads, measuring portions of circular tracks, and determining the length of an arch in construction. We will learn how to calculate arc length when given a radius and central angle, as well as how to find the radius or angle measure if the arc length is known. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to apply the arc length formula, solve mathematical and real-world problems, and understand the relationships between angles, radii, and circles.


Finding Arc Length

Arc length is a fraction of the total circumference of a circle. The formula for finding arc length will use the central angle given in degrees or radians.

\(L=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot 2\pi r\)

where:

  • \(L\) is the arc length,
  • \(\theta\) is the central angle in degrees,
  • \(r\) is the radius of the circle



Example 1

In \(\odot A\), \(m\angle BAC=106^\circ\) and the length of the radius is 4.

Find the length of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\).

Solution:

  • To find the length of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\), we will use the arc length formula \(L=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot 2\pi r\).
  • We are given the measure of the central angle, \(\angle BAC\).  Thus, \(\theta=106\).
  • We are given the length of the radius. Thus, \(r=4\).
  • \(L=\frac{106}{360}\cdot 2\pi (4) \implies L=\frac{106}{360}(8\pi) \implies L=\frac{8\pi \cdot 106}{360} \implies L=\frac{848\pi}{360}\)
  • We can reduce our fraction \(\frac{848}{360}\) to be \(\frac{106}{45}\).
  • Therefore, the EXACT answer for the length of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\) is \(L=\frac{106\pi}{45}\).
  • If asked to approximate, we would multiple 106 by \(\pi\), then divide by 45. Thus \(L\approx 7.4002\).



Example 2

In \(\odot A\), \(m\angle BAC=70^\circ\) and the length of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\) is \(\frac{35\pi}{18}\).

Find the length of the radius.

Solution:

  • To find the length of the radius, we will use the arc length formula \(L=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\cdot 2\pi r\).
  • We substitute in the values we know and solve for the one we do not know.
  • \(\frac{35\pi}{18}=\frac{70}{360}\cdot 2\pi r\)
    \(\frac{35\pi}{18}=\frac{7\cdot 2\pi}{36}r\)
    \(\frac{35\pi}{18}=\frac{14\pi}{36}r\) - we will use the reciprocal of the fraction \(\frac{14\pi}{36}\) to multiply to the other side to isolate \(r\).
    \(\frac{36}{14\pi}\cdot \frac{35\pi}{18} = r\)
  • Now, we can cancel out the \(\pi\) that is the numerator of one fraction with the one in the denominator of the other fraction. Then you can multiply and reduce or reduce then multiply.
  • \(r=\frac{1260}{252} \implies r=5\)
  • We find that the length of the radius is 5 units.



Example 3

In \(\odot A\), the length of the radius is 6.5 units, and the length of \(\overset{\frown}{BC}\) is \(\approx 15.882\).

Find the approximate measure of \(\angle BAC\) rounded to the nearest whole degree.

Solution:

  • To find the approximate measure of \(\angle BAC\), we will substitute the values we know into the arc length formula.
  • \(15.882=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot 2\pi (6.5)\)
    \(15.882=\frac{\theta}{360}(13\pi)\)
    \(15.882\div 13\pi =\frac{\theta}{360}\)
    \(360\cdot (15.882\div 13\pi)=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot 360\)
  • At this point, you will want to use your calculator. Be sure to type in the whole part on the left in at once and not in pieces so that the answer is only rounded once!
  • We find the approximate measure of \(\angle BAC\) is \(140^\circ\).



Example 4

A circular track has a radius of 25 meters. A runner covers 40 meters along the curved path.

What is the approximate measure of the central angle? Round your answer to the nearest tenth of a degree.

Solution:

  • To find the approximate measure of the central angle, we will substitute the values we know into the arc length formula.
  • The distance the runner covered on the path is the arc length, 40, and the radius is 25.
  • \(40=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot 2\pi (25)\)
    \(40=\frac{\theta}{360}(50\pi)\)
    \(40\div 50\pi =\frac{\theta}{360}\)
    \(360\cdot (40\div 50\pi)=\frac{\theta}{360}\cdot 360\)
    \(\theta = 360(40\div 50\pi)\)
  • At this point, you will want to use your calculator. Be sure to type in the whole part on the left in at once and not in pieces so that the answer is only rounded once!
  • We find the measure of the central angle of the circular track covered is \(91.7^\circ\).



Conclusion

Arc length is a fundamental concept in geometry and real-world applications such as road construction, amusement park rides, and architectural design. By understanding how to calculate arc length, as well as how to find the radius or central angle when arc length is known, we can solve a variety of problems involving circular motion and design. Whether planning a highway curve or determining how far a Ferris wheel seat moves, applying the arc length formula helps us make precise calculations and informed decisions.
