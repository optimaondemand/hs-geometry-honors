📚 VOCABULARY

3-Dimensional Figure: A solid shape that has length, width, and height, taking up space in three dimensions.

Apex: The highest point of a cone or pyramid, where the lateral faces or curved surface meet.

Area: The measure of space enclosed within a two-dimensional shape, expressed in square units.

Base: The face of a 3D figure that serves as a foundation and is used to determine surface area and volume.

Cone: A 3D shape with a circular base and a curved surface that tapers to a single point called the apex.

Cylinder: A 3D shape with two parallel circular bases connected by a curved surface.

Face: A flat surface of a 3D figure, such as the bases and lateral faces of prisms and pyramids.

Height: The perpendicular distance from the base to the top of a 3D figure.

Lateral Area: The sum of the areas of only the lateral faces (not including the base) of a 3D shape.

Lateral Face: A face of a 3D figure that is not a base, typically found in prisms and pyramids.

Net: A two-dimensional pattern that can be folded to form a 3D shape.

Perimeter: The total length of the boundary of a 2D shape, used in some surface area calculations.

Prism: A 3D figure with two parallel, congruent bases and rectangular lateral faces.

Pyramid: A 3D figure with a polygonal base and triangular lateral faces that meet at an apex.

Radius: A line segment from the center of a circle to any point on the circle’s circumference, used in circular base calculations.

Slant Height: The distance from the apex of a pyramid or cone to the edge of the base, measured along the lateral surface.

Sphere: A perfectly round 3D shape where all points are equidistant from the center.

Surface Area: The total area of all faces or curved surfaces of a 3D figure.

Total Surface Area: The sum of the lateral area and the area of all bases in a 3D figure.

Vertex: A point where edges or faces of a 3D figure meet.

💡 EXPLORING THE MATH

In today’s lesson, we will explore the concept of surface area and how to calculate it for various 3D figures, including pyramids and prisms. Surface area represents the total area of all the faces or curved surfaces of a shape. By understanding how to break these solids into their individual faces or curved surfaces, we can connect the idea of nets to their respective formulas. This skill is useful in many real-world applications, such as construction, packaging, and manufacturing. By the end of this lesson, you will be able to identify and apply the correct formula for surface area, analyze 3D solids, and solve real-world problems involving surface area.


Understanding Surface Area Conceptually

Surface area is the total area that covers a 3D shape. If we imagine unfolding a solid into a flat representation, we create a net, which helps visualize and calculate the total surface area.
Each type of solid has a specific set of faces or curved surfaces, and we use different formulas to calculate their total surface area.

Here are examples of a Right Rectangular Prism and its net and a Right Rectangular Pyramid and its net.

The net helps you to see the 2D shapes that fold to make the 3D solid. The surface area is just the area of all the surfaces or the 2D shapes.

In the rectangular prism, there are 2 types of each rectangle; 2 rectangles for the top and bottom, 2 rectangles for the front and back, and 2 rectangles for the sides.

In the rectangular pyramid, there is 1 rectangle, and 4 triangles; 2 identical triangles for the front and back, and 2 identical triangles for the sides.



Surface Area Formulas for Prisms and Pyramids

Prism Pyramid

\(\Large SA=2B+Ph\)

Where:

  • \(B\) is the area of the base
  • \(P\) is the perimeter of the base
  • \(h\) is the height of the prism

Explanation: The total surface area is the sum of the area of both bases (\(2B\)) and the lateral area (\(Ph\)), where \(P\) is the perimeter of the base.

  • The bases of a prism can be any polygon - triangle, rectangle, square, parallelogram, pentagon, hexagon, or octagon.
  • The lateral faces of a prism are always rectangles.

\(\Large SA=B+\frac{1}{2}Pl\)

Where:

  • \(B\) is the area of the base
  • \(P\) is the perimeter of the base
  • \(l\) is the slant height of the lateral face

Explanation: The total surface area is the sum of the base area (\(B\)) and the lateral faces, which are triangles (\(\frac{1}{2}Pl\)), where \(l\) is the slant height.

  • The base of a pyramid can be any polygon, the same as a prism.
  • The lateral faces are always triangles.
  • The slant height is the triangular face's height, not the pyramid's height.
  • If the base is not a regular polygon (like a rectangle), then there could be more than one slant height.

**NOTE** - The formula for a pyramid only works if the base is a regular polygon; i.e. a square, an equilateral triangle, a regular hexagon, or a regular octagon.

This ensures that all sides of the base are equal which means all slant heights of each lateral face are equal.


Example 1

Find the surface area of the right rectangular pyramid shown. Round to the nearest hundredths if necessary.

Solution:

  • To find the surface area of a rectangular pyramid, we need to think about it conceptually using the sum of the area of all the faces. The formula will not work for this since the base is not a regular polygon or a square.
  • Since the base is a rectangle, there are 2 different slant heights since there are 2 different sized triangles. There is a skinny triangle for the sides of the pyramid and a wider triangle for the front and back.
  • To find the surface area, we will find the area of all the faces. There are 2 triangles that represent the sides. Then there are 2 triangles that represent the front and back. Finally, there is a rectangle that represents the base.
  • We can begin by finding the area of the triangles that represent the sides. The slant height on the side is the height of the triangular face. We can find the area of 1 and multiply by 2 to represent both of them since they are the same.
    • \(A_{SideTri}=\frac{1}{2}(3)(\sqrt{34}) \implies A_{SideTri}=\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{34}\)
    • Now, we can multiply by 2 for both sides - \(A_{SideTri}=2(\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{34}) \implies A_{SideTri}=3\sqrt{34}\)
    • We will leave the answer in the exact form until we are ready to calculate the total surface area.
  • Next, we will calculate the area for the front and back triangles. Again, we will multiply by 2 to account for both.
    • \(A_{Triangles}=2\cdot \frac{1}{2}(6)(\frac{\sqrt{109}}{2}) \implies A_{Triangles}=6(\frac{\sqrt{109}}{2}) \implies A_{Triangles}=3\sqrt{109}\)
  • Last, we will calculate the area of the rectangular base.
    • \(A_{Base}=(3)(6) \implies A_{Base}=18\)
  • To find the total surface area, we add together all the areas of the faces.
  • \(SA=3\sqrt{34}+3\sqrt{109}+18\)
    • This is the exact surface area. We will use the calculator and plug that whole expression in at once to find the approximate surface area.

The total approximate surface area of the pyramid is \(SA\approx 66.81\;units^2\).


Now, let's take a look at a square pyramid and use the formula to compare the two methods.

Example 2

Find the surface area of the square pyramid. Round to the nearest tenths if necessary.

Solution:

  • This pyramid has a square base and all sides are equal. This means it will only have 1 slant height. Therefore, we can use the formula for the surface area of a pyramid for this example.
  • To find the surface area, we will use the formula \(SA=B+\frac{1}{2}Pl\).
  • To use the formula, we need to first find the area of the base. We will use the area of a square formula from Lesson 8.01a - \(A=s^2\) where s is the side length.
    • \(A=(6)^2 \implies A=36\)
  • Next, we need to find the perimeter of the base for the \(P\).
    • \(P=4\cdot 6 \implies P=24\)
  • Now that we have the values needed for the formula, we can substitute them to find the surface area.
  • Total Surface Area is \(SA=36+\frac{1}{2}(24)(\sqrt{58}) \implies SA=36+12(\sqrt{58})\).
    • This is the exact value. Now we will use a calculator to find the approximate value.
  • Approximate Total Surface Area is \(SA\approx 127.4\;units^2\).



Example 3

Find the total surface area of the rectangular prism shown. Round to the nearest hundredths if necessary.

Solution:

Method 1: Conceptual

  • To find the surface area of this prism, we will calculate the area of all the faces and add them together.
    • There are two 3x4 rectangles for the sides - \(A_{sides}=2(3\cdot 4) \implies A_{sides}=24\)
    • There are two 3x7 rectangles for the front and back - \(A_{front/back}=2(3\cdot 7) \implies A_{front/back}=42\)
    • There are two 4x7 rectangles for the top and bottom - \(A_{top/bottom}=2(4\cdot 7) \implies A_{top/bottom}=56\)
  • Total Surface Area - \(SA=24+42+56 \implies SA=122\; units^2\)


Method 2: Formula

  • We can also use the surface area formula given above - \(SA=2B+Ph\).
    • \(B\) is the area of the base; \(B=4\cdot 7 \implies B=28\)
    • \(P\) is the perimeter of the base; \(P=2\cdot 4+2\cdot 7 \implies P=8+14 \implies P=22\)
    • \(h\) is the height of the prism; \(h=3\)
    • Now, we can substitute all those values into the formula.
  • Total Surface Area - \(SA=2(28)+(22)(3) \implies SA=56+66 \implies SA=122\; units^2\)

Notice that we got the same answer with both methods. There are 2 ways to find the surface area and both methods will always give you the same answer. So, it is your choice how to find the surface area for which method makes the most sense to you!



Example 4

Find the surface area of the right pentagonal prism shown.

Solution:

To find the surface area of this prism we can use either method like the previous example. Either method we use, we will need to remind ourselves of the formula for the area of a polygon from Lesson 8.01b - \(A=\frac{1}{2}aP\) where \(a\) is the apothem of the polygon and \(P\) is the perimeter of the polygon.

Method 1:

  • We can begin by finding the area of the pentagonal base and multiplying it by 2 for the top and bottom.
    • First we need the perimeter of the base \(P=4\cdot 5 \implies P=20\)
    • \(A_{bases}=2(\frac{1}{2}(2.75)(20)) \implies A_{bases}=2(27.5) \implies A_{bases}=55\)
  • Next we can find the area of the rectangular lateral face. There are 5 of these faces. So, we will multiply the area by 5.
    • \(A_{faces}=5(6\cdot 4) \implies A_{faces}=5(24) \implies A_{faces}=120\)
  • Total Surface area is the sum of those two areas.
    • \(SA=55+120 \implies SA=175\;units^2\)


Method 2:

  • We will use the surface area formula - \(SA=2B+Ph\)
  • First, we need to find the area of the base using the area of a polygon formula - \(A=\frac{1}{2}aP\).
    • To use this formula, we need to first find the perimeter of the base - \(P=4\cdot 5 \implies P=20\) 
    • \(B=\frac{1}{2}(2.75)(20) \implies B=27.5\)
  • Since we already found the perimeter of the base for the area of the base formula, we can now substitute our values into the surface area formula.
  • Total Surface area is \(SA=2\cdot 27.5 + 20\cdot 6 \implies SA=55+120 \implies SA=175\; units^2\).



Example 5

Find the surface area of the right hexagonal pyramid shown.

Solution:

To find the surface area of this pyramid we can use either method like the previous examples. Either method we use, we will need to remind ourselves of the formula for the area of a polygon from Lesson 8.01b - \(A=\frac{1}{2}aP\) where \(a\) is the apothem of the polygon and \(P\) is the perimeter of the polygon.

Method 1:

  • We can begin by finding the area of the hexagonal base.
    • First we need the perimeter of the base \(P=6\cdot 5 \implies P=30\)
    • \(A_{base}=\frac{1}{2}(4.33)(30) \implies A_{base}=64.95\)
  • Next we can find the area of the triangular lateral face. There are 6 of these faces. So, we will multiply the area by 6.
    • Notice that the slant height of the pyramid is also the height of the triangular face. 
    • \(A_{faces}=6(\frac{1}{2}(5)(5.89)) \implies A_{faces}=6(14.725) \implies A_{faces}=88.35\)
  • Total Surface area is the sum of those two areas.
    • \(SA=64.95+88.35 \implies SA=153.3\;units^2\)


Method 2: Formula
We can use the formula for a pyramid since the base is a regular hexagon where all the sides are equal and there is only 1 slant height.

  • We will use the surface area formula - \(SA=B+\frac{1}{2}Pl\)
  • First, we need to find the area of the base using the area of a polygon formula - \(A=\frac{1}{2}aP\).
    • To use this formula, we need to first find the perimeter of the base - \(P=6\cdot 5 \implies P=30\) 
    • \(B=\frac{1}{2}(4.33)(30) \implies B=64.95\)
  • Since we already found the perimeter of the base for the area of the base formula, we can now substitute our values into the surface area formula.
    • Remember that \(l\) is the slant height of the pyramid which is the height of one of the lateral faces - \(l=5.89\).
  • Total Surface area is \(SA=64.95 + \frac{1}{2}(30)(5.89) \implies SA=64.95+88.35 \implies SA=153.3\; units^2\).



Conclusion

Understanding surface area is essential in geometry and real-world applications such as construction, product packaging, and engineering. By breaking down 3D figures into their nets, we can visualize and calculate their total surface area using formulas. Whether determining the amount of material needed for a water tank, a gift box, or a balloon covering, applying the correct surface area formula ensures accurate and practical results. With these skills, you can confidently tackle real-world problems involving pyramids and prisms.
